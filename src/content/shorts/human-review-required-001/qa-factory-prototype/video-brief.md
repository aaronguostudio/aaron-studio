# QA Factory — 8-Second Prototype Brief

## Target Audience

AI-native builders who recognize the gap between a green automated checklist and the instant human reaction, “nope.” They have already seen enough AI explainers and do not need another narrated lesson.

## Desired Emotion

Confident recognition, an awkward half-beat, then a clean physical laugh.

## Core Promise

In eight seconds and without narration, turn the abstract claim “technical QA is not human judgment” into one physical gag: a real AI-made video clears a miniature QA factory, the factory celebrates, and one human hand drops it through a trapdoor marked `NOPE`.

## Title/Thumbnail Expectation

There is no title card. Frame zero already shows the real old video as a product tile on a working paper-and-cardboard conveyor. `QA` exists only as a factory sign. The visual contract is action, not presentation.

## High-Shock Facts

- The real source video passed its recorded technical QA.
- The encoded source is 1920×1080 at 60 fps.
- Its encoded mix measured approximately -16.2 LUFS.
- A full decode completed without errors.
- Aaron still rejected the result on human-review grounds: the music felt bad and the recreated demo looked fake.

## Hook Type

Physical contradiction. Target: builders who trust measurable checks. Transformation: `PASSED` becomes `NOPE` without an explanatory sentence. Stakes: a technically valid artifact can still fail taste and trust.

## Story Structure

One continuous miniature factory space:

1. `0.00–4.50s` — the real video tile travels through three QA checkpoints; green lamps, a `PASSED` stamp, and restrained paper-confetti celebrate it.
2. `4.50–6.00s` — a paper human hand enters the review station; at `4.60s` the music stops sample-hard and the trapdoor latch clunks. The silent hold is the setup.
3. `6.00–8.00s` — the trapdoor opens, the product falls out of the factory, and the red physical stencil `NOPE` owns the final hold.

## Retention Beat Map

- `0.00s`: real video tile and complete factory already visible.
- `0.30s`: conveyor starts.
- `0.70s`: first scan and `1080P` lamp.
- `1.45s`: `60 FPS` lamp.
- `2.20s`: `0 ERRORS` lamp.
- `3.15s`: `PASSED` stamp lands.
- `3.45s`: tiny paper celebration.
- `4.50s`: human hand enters the station.
- `4.60s`: music cuts dead; latch clunks.
- `6.00s`: trapdoor flips open.
- `6.18s`: product begins falling.
- `6.75s`: product is gone; `NOPE` is fully readable.
- `7.20–8.00s`: silent punchline hold.

## What The Video Adds

The earlier short explained the distinction. This prototype makes the distinction happen to an object. Sound, timing, material, and cause-and-effect carry the idea; the viewer does not have to read a thesis or listen to Aaron’s cloned voice.

## Banned Phrases

- If only the machine likes it
- Human review required
- Taste, trust, truth
- Here’s the lesson
- AI passed QA
- Any explanatory subtitle, quote card, scorecard, or voiceover

## Ending

The real video tile disappears into the open chute. Only the physical `NOPE` stencil and the embarrassed factory remain. No end card and no CTA.

## Audit Status

- Story flow: locked as one setup, one interruption, and one payoff.
- Comprehension: must work with audio muted; the score only improves the timing.
- Speed to value: real artifact and factory action are visible at frame zero.
- User approval: Aaron approved making the `QA Factory` prototype on 2026-08-04.
- Production status: planning only; the `custom-signature` scene and `three-d-flip` motion are experimental and require prototype review.
