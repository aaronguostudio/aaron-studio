# Director Memo — QA Factory

This is not a shorter explainer. It is one visual sentence with a setup, a reaction, and a consequence.

At frame zero, the viewer is already looking down into a complete miniature cardboard factory. The actual failed video is playing inside a product tile on the center conveyor. Three physical QA stations approve it, a heavy `PASSED` stamp lands, and the surrounding machine performs a tiny, self-satisfied celebration.

The same tile reaches a bay marked `HUMAN REVIEW`. A paper hand enters from the top of the frame. At exactly `4.600s`, it touches the reject control: the music disappears at the sample boundary and the latch clunks. Nothing explains the verdict. The factory, the hand, and the approved product hold in awkward silence.

At `6.000s`, the trapdoor opens. The approved product falls through it. The only surviving copy is the red stencil inside the chute: `NOPE`.

The comedy rhythm is confidence → interruption → delayed consequence. The 1.4-second silent wait between the latch and the opening matters more than another visual flourish. Do not shorten it merely because the frame is quiet.

The generated factory background and paper hand are theatrical world-building only. They are never framed as proof. The real old video is the sole evidence asset, remains recognizable inside the product tile, keeps its original disclosure treatment where legible, and contributes no source audio.

The frame remains one locked top-down space. Labels are attached to machines, stamps, lamps, or chutes. There are no floating headlines, captions, quote cards, score panels, simulated app windows, cloned narration, reaction memes, or explanatory ending.

`custom-signature` and `three-d-flip` remain experimental. The production-safe fallback is a five-state `image-sequence` from the same 9:16 composition with the same audio. The signature should not be promoted or expanded into a full series until the encoded prototype passes sound-off comprehension, normal-speed comedy timing, slow-motion geometry review, and Aaron’s taste gate.
