## Product Promise

Replace the voice-led slide language of the first film with eight seconds of silent physical comedy. The machine’s certainty is expressed as factory behavior; the human verdict is expressed as one hand and one trapdoor.

## Direction Decision

Selected: **Miniature Factory Slapstick**. Aaron explicitly approved the proposed `QA Factory` prototype after rejecting the prior voice-led, PowerPoint-like form.

Rejected for this test:

- A funnier substitute narrator; it would preserve the explainer structure.
- An animated scorecard or talent-show panel; it would still read as a sequence of slides.
- Quote typography; the joke should happen before the viewer has to read a sentence.

## Visual Spine

- One locked 1080×1920 top-down miniature factory world for the entire runtime.
- Warm worn paper, cardboard, black work surface, faded green machine details, green QA lamps, and one red rejection state.
- The center conveyor is the permanent spatial axis.
- The real old video plays inside a physical product tile; its source audio is muted.
- `QA`, `1080P`, `60 FPS`, `0 ERRORS`, `PASSED`, `HUMAN REVIEW`, and `NOPE` appear only as signs, bulbs, stamps, or stencils attached to the factory.
- No overlay headline, captions, quote cards, browser chrome, or simulated chat interface.

## Motion Language

- Camera: locked. No orbit, zoom, shake, or simulated handheld behavior.
- Product tile: frame-driven vertical conveyor travel on one axis.
- QA: three small lamp state changes, then one mechanically heavy paper stamp.
- Human review: one paper hand enters, pauses, and touches the reject control.
- Payoff: the registered experimental `three-d-flip` recipe drives the trapdoor hinge; the product then falls on a bounded central path.
- Curves: conveyor linear; prop entrances `Easing.bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`; trapdoor `Easing.bezier(0.45, 0, 0.55, 1)`; falling tile ease-in.
- Deliberately rejected: bounce, elastic overshoot, confetti cannon, per-letter animation, rotating labels, camera push, particle field, and a second joke after `NOPE`.

- `prototype_required`: true.

## Questions The Prototype Must Answer

1. Is the story readable with the phone muted?
2. Does the sample-hard stop at `4.600s` create a laugh rather than feel like an audio error?
3. Does the real source video remain recognizable inside the physical tile?
4. Does the image-generated world feel like theatrical paper craft rather than fake evidence?
5. Is `NOPE` visible long enough to land without an explanatory end card?
