## Post copy

Why can the same model behave like a different product in two different environments?

In one, it can work for hours, recover from a crash, verify a change, and deliver safely.

In the other, it guesses at the repository, loses its progress, and mistakes “looks right” for “done.”

The difference is often the harness around the model:

- a task contract that defines observable success;
- a context map that points to current sources of truth;
- scoped tools inside an isolated environment;
- durable state beyond one context window;
- verification that checks the environment, not just the final prose;
- permissions that bound authority;
- recovery that resumes without duplicating side effects.

That is the mental model I use for Harness Engineering:

The model supplies capability.  
The harness turns it into dependable work.

It is also why an agent framework is not the finished system. A framework gives you parts. The harness is the task-specific runtime you assemble. Harness Engineering is the practice of deciding which guarantees belong in context, tools, code, evaluators, permissions, and durable state.
