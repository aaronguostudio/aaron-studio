# Editorial Brief

## Primary reader

A curious non-specialist who has visited Athens, plans to visit, or has rediscovered Greek mythology through travel and wants a story that is vivid without pretending uncertainty does not exist.

## Reader pain

The familiar contest is easy to remember but hard to trust. Popular retellings often merge the gifts, judges, city naming, sculptures, and later traditions into one seamless “original” story.

## Reader job and promise

**Job:** understand what the Acropolis actually preserves, what ancient writers say, and where modern reconstruction begins.

**Promise:** after reading, the reader will be able to look at the west pediment and Erechtheion as connected parts of one story while recognizing which details belong to artifact, text, scholarship, or imagination.

## Sharp thesis

Athena wins in the myth, but Athens preserves Poseidon's claim beside hers; the sacred landscape remembers the contest as coexistence, not erasure.

## Concrete opening

Standing on the Acropolis with family, Aaron encounters a contradiction: much of the sculpture is gone, but the story still feels present.

## Original contribution

Most beginner retellings stop at “Athena won.” This chapter follows the aftermath into the Erechtheion, where Athena's cult, Poseidon-Erechtheus, the olive, the salt water, and the trident marks were held within one architectural complex. The contribution is not a new historical claim; it is a source-bounded reading of what Athens chose to remember.

## Why Aaron can write this

Aaron is not claiming classical expertise. His authority comes from the real visitor's question, the willingness to show the research boundary, and the ability to turn a complicated evidence trail into a readable interactive experience.

## Authority and scope boundary

- The contest is presented as an ancient tradition, not a verified historical or supernatural event.
- No single judging procedure is treated as universal canon.
- Pseudo-Apollodorus provides the fullest narrative sequence used here, but it is a later mythographic account.
- The Erechtheion proves that cults and sacred tokens associated with both deities occupied the complex; it does not by itself prove a conscious political program of reconciliation.
- The generated reconstruction does not reproduce the exact original pose, cast, color, or surface.

## Evidence needed

- Institutional description of the Parthenon pediments and surviving Athena/Poseidon fragments.
- Pausanias 1.24.5 for the west-pediment subject.
- Herodotus 8.55 and Pausanias 1.26.5 for the olive, salt water, and trident testimony.
- Pseudo-Apollodorus, *Library* 3.14.1 for one fuller version of arrival, gifts, witnesses, judges, and verdict.
- Acropolis Museum description of the Erechtheion's divided cult spaces and incorporated sacred tokens.
- Meyer 2018 for the role of fragments and the 1674 drawing in composition-level reconstruction.

## Credible counterargument

Athena clearly remains the patron deity, and a multi-cult sanctuary does not make the two gods equal. The chapter should not dissolve the victory into vague balance. Its narrower claim is that Poseidon's defeated claim remained materially and ritually present.

## Reusable reader lens

For each mythic scene, ask four questions in order:

1. What survives?
2. What do named ancient texts attest?
3. What can scholarship reconstruct?
4. What has the artist supplied?

## Distribution and product hypothesis

The evidence-to-reconstruction visual should earn the first interaction; the “winner who remains” thesis should earn continued reading. Success is measured by episode opens, interaction completion, 75%/100% scroll depth, source-link use, and return clicks to the season hub—not by generic social reach.

## Recent workflow lesson applied

Keep one thesis and one primary visual relationship. The chapter will not add a second framework, deity family tree, map, or force-directed graph before the core story works.

## Kill criteria

Stop or reframe the chapter if the article:

- presents the later narrative as the unique original version;
- claims the Erechtheion proves intentional political reconciliation;
- lets the artistic reconstruction appear more authoritative than the surviving fragments and linked sources;
- needs an AI analogy to justify why the story matters.
