# Chapter Idea — Athena and Poseidon

## Raw topic

Use the Parthenon west pediment and the nearby Erechtheion to tell the contest between Athena and Poseidon as an artifact-first story.

## Reader pain

Most introductions flatten the myth into a clean plot: two gods offer gifts, Athena wins, and Athens receives her name. That version is memorable, but it hides two things a curious visitor needs in order to trust the story:

- the surviving sculpture is fragmentary;
- ancient accounts do not give one single, complete script.

## Initial thesis

Athena wins in the story, but Athens does not erase Poseidon. The sacred landscape keeps both claims visible, turning the myth from a winner-takes-all contest into a memory of powers the city had to hold together.

## Why this chapter now

This series began during Aaron's family trip through Greece. The Acropolis made the mythology feel less like a list of gods and more like a world still embedded in stone, place, and ritual memory.
