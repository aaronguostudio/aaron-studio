# Canon Note — Chapter 1

## Canonical Editorial Contribution

The chapter establishes **victory without erasure** as the governing interpretation of the Athena–Poseidon contest: Athena wins the attested verdict, while Poseidon's claim remains present in the sacred landscape, cult spaces, and remembered signs of the Acropolis. This is not a claim that the gods held equal status or that ancient Athens practiced a modern politics of reconciliation.

## Version Discipline

- Greek mythology has no single universal continuity.
- Pausanias identifies the west-pediment subject but does not supply a complete contest script.
- Herodotus records the olive and the “sea” as Athenian testimonies and separately records the olive's regrowth after the Persian fire.
- Pseudo-Apollodorus supplies one later ancient sequence involving arrival, tokens, witnesses, arbiters, and verdict. It must remain labeled as one version, with competing traditions about the arbiters kept visible.
- No future chapter should silently merge these accounts into one supposedly definitive narrative.

## Four Evidence Layers

Every reuse of this chapter's material must preserve the series' four evidence statuses:

1. `artifact-documented` — surviving objects, fragments, sites, or institutional catalogue records.
2. `text-attested` — a named ancient tradition, not proof that a supernatural or historical event occurred.
3. `scholarly-reconstruction` — a reasoned reconstruction whose uncertainty remains visible.
4. `artistic-interpretation` — a creative completion carrying no evidentiary claim.

These layers may coexist in one experience, but they must never be presented as interchangeable.

## Inference Boundary

The olive's new growth after the Persian destruction is text-attested in Herodotus. Reading that report as a story of **urban continuity, recovery, or a wounded city's belief in its future** is an authorial inference. It is not an explicit ancient doctrine and must remain phrased as interpretation.

Likewise, “victory did not require erasure” is the chapter's evidence-based reading of the surviving spatial and textual record, not a quotation from an ancient source.

## Reusable Pattern

This chapter establishes an **artifact-first** pattern for the series:

1. Begin with a surviving place or object and state what is missing.
2. Add named ancient accounts without forcing them into one continuity.
3. Introduce scholarly reconstruction only where it clarifies the surviving evidence.
4. Label artistic completion plainly.
5. End with a human interpretation whose inferential status remains visible.

This pattern may be reused by later chapters, while their claims, entities, and sources must be researched and registered independently.
