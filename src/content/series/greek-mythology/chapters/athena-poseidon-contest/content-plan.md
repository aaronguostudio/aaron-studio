# Content Plan: Athena and Poseidon

## Voice check

**Positioning:** A curious builder learning to read myth through surviving places and evidence.

**Voice rule:** Use first person only for the Acropolis encounter and the final changed perception. Keep the history source-led. Do not adopt a lecturer's voice.

**Personal anchor:** Aaron's family trip to the Acropolis and the feeling that an incomplete monument could still hold an unusually vivid world.

## Hook

The sculpture is mostly gone, yet the contest remains easy to picture. That contradiction invites the reader to move from stone to text and then to the space where both gods remained present.

## Core thesis

Athena wins in the myth, but Athens preserves Poseidon's claim beside hers; the sacred landscape remembers victory without erasing the defeated power.

## Story movement

1. **A story with its center missing** — family visit, ruined pediment, surviving fragments, named subject.
2. **Two signs on the rock** — Herodotus and Pausanias establish olive, salt water, and trident marks as Athenian testimony.
3. **One version of the contest** — Pseudo-Apollodorus supplies the fuller sequence and simultaneously reveals variant judges.
4. **Athena wins** — keep the verdict clear; avoid historical etymology claims.
5. **Poseidon remains** — walk to the Erechtheion and show the two cult spaces and incorporated tokens.
6. **What the city chose to remember** — Aaron's interpretation: memory can preserve unresolved powers without flattening them into a tie.
7. **From evidence to reconstruction** — connect the prose to the drag interaction and close on a changed way of seeing.

## Visual role

The existing evidence/reconstruction slider carries the argument. No additional family tree, map, or decorative deity portrait is needed for Chapter 1.
