# Red-Team Review

## Issue 1 — The thesis could sound like modern reconciliation

**Risk:** “Athens kept both gods” may be heard as a claim that ancient Athens consciously practiced pluralist conflict resolution.

**Revision:** Added explicit language that shared cult space does not prove a modern program of tolerance or political reconciliation. Narrowed the claim to material and ritual preservation of Poseidon's defeated claim.

## Issue 2 — The familiar gift story could be presented as universal canon

**Risk:** A smooth narrative would hide the difference between Herodotus, Pausanias, and Pseudo-Apollodorus.

**Revision:** Introduced Pseudo-Apollodorus as “one later ancient account” and used the text's own variant list to refuse a definitive judging procedure.

## Issue 3 — A personal scene could invent travel detail

**Risk:** Weather, conversation, or children's reactions would create false lived evidence.

**Revision:** Kept only the user-supplied fact of a family visit and a clearly personal act of mentally repairing the ruin.

## Issue 4 — The olive-resprouting passage risks religious sensationalism

**Risk:** It could be written as proof of a miracle.

**Revision:** Framed it as evidence for how Athenians narrated continuity, not evidence that a supernatural event occurred.

## Issue 5 — Symbolic interpretation could outrun sources

**Risk:** “Agriculture versus sea power” and “reason versus nature” are attractive but unsupported by the registered evidence.

**Revision:** Named them as tempting readings, then declined to make them the article's verdict.

## Issue 6 — The interaction could imply a true restoration

**Risk:** A polished full-color image may visually overpower the disclaimer.

**Revision:** The conclusion explicitly says the slider is not a time machine and explains both images' interpretive status.

## Issue 7 — The middle could become a source catalogue

**Risk:** Moving source by source would flatten the story.

**Revision:** Organized sources by narrative job: missing center, tokens, one full account, surviving sacred space, reconstruction boundary.

## Issue 8 — The ending could merely repeat the thesis

**Risk:** “Athena won, Poseidon stayed” can sound like a slogan without payoff.

**Revision:** Landed on a changed way of reading the Acropolis: conflict itself became part of the city's memory.

## Substantive revision completed

The draft originally moved directly from tokens to verdict. It now adds the Pseudo-Apollodorus version as a clearly bounded narrative bridge, then makes the Erechtheion—not symbolic gift comparison—the central turn of the article.

## Decision

**PASS for prose polish.** No factual gap blocks the argument; wording must continue to preserve source and inference boundaries.
