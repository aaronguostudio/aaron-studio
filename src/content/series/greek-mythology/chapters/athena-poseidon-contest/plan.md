# Blog Plan: Athena and Poseidon — Who Should Guard Athens?

## Meta

- **Author:** Aaron Guo
- **Target:** Greek Mythology special on aaronguo.com (EN + ZH)
- **Tone:** Warm, story-first, source-aware, visually literate; no teacherly survey or forced business analogy
- **Length:** 1,400–1,800 words EN; 2,200–3,200 Chinese characters
- **Audience:** Curious non-specialists, families, and Greece travelers
- **CTA:** Return to *Athens: Gods in Stone*

## Hook

Standing on the Acropolis with his family, Aaron notices that the center of the famous contest is mostly missing. The story, however, is not.

## Thesis

Athena wins in the myth, but Athens preserves Poseidon's claim beside hers; the sacred landscape remembers victory without erasure.

## Personal anchor

The family trip that made Greek mythology feel present in places and objects rather than remote in a textbook.

## Outline

### Part 1: The story survives its missing center

- Begin with the Acropolis contradiction.
- Name the west-pediment subject through Pausanias and the museum.
- Describe only the surviving Athena and Poseidon fragments.
- Establish that the complete image on the page is a reconstruction.

### Part 2: Two witnesses, not yet a full plot

- Herodotus: olive tree and “sea” as Athenian witnesses.
- Pausanias: salt-water cistern, wave sound, and trident outline.
- Let the reader feel the physicality of the tokens without presenting them as proof of a miracle.

### Part 3: One ancient account fills in the action

- Introduce Pseudo-Apollodorus explicitly as one later account.
- Poseidon arrives and strikes the rock; Athena plants the olive.
- Zeus appoints twelve gods; Cecrops testifies; Athena receives the land.
- Use the text's mention of other arbiters to show why there is no single canonical script.

### Part 4: Athena wins—and Poseidon stays

- Do not blur the verdict.
- Move physically and narratively to the Erechtheion.
- East room: Athena; lower west room: Poseidon-Erechtheus and other shrines.
- The building also incorporated the contest's sacred tokens.

### Part 5: A city remembers more than its winner

- State the article's interpretation as interpretation.
- The point is not equal status or modern tolerance.
- Athens preserved the force it did not choose as patron.
- This makes the myth more interesting than a moral fable about the “better gift.”

### Part 6: Dragging between evidence and imagination

- Return to the page's split image.
- Explain what the 1674 drawing and fragments can support.
- Name complete poses, faces, color, and atmosphere as artistic choices.
- End with Aaron's changed view of the Acropolis: not a frozen pantheon, but a layered argument kept in stone.
