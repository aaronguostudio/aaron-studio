# Content Plan: The Erechtheion — One Building, Many Memories

## Voice check

**Positioning:** A curious visitor learning to read a sacred building through architecture, named ancient traditions, and visible uncertainty.

**Voice rule:** Begin with recognition and curiosity, then let the building teach the structure. Explain names in plain language. Never sound as if the article has solved questions that classical scholarship still holds open.

**Personal anchor:** Aaron's family trip to the Acropolis and his fascination with the mythic world embedded in its monuments. Use only the supplied fact of the trip and fascination; do not invent scene details.

## Hook / opening

The Erechtheion is easy to recognize and difficult to explain. Six women hold up one porch, but walk around the building and its sides refuse to match: levels shift, entrances multiply, and the cult image that mattered most is no longer there.

The opening question is not “why did the architect fail to make it symmetrical?” It is “what did this building have to keep?”

## Core argument / thesis

The surviving form and institutional reconstructions show the Erechtheion adapting to uneven ground while accommodating several cults, ancestral places, and contest tokens. The chapter's interpretation is that its asymmetry makes accumulated sacred memory visible: Athens did not preserve one clean story here, but many obligations held in proximity.

## Narrative and evidence contract

- Every section should have one physical anchor.
- A section may receive one evidence badge only if all its material claims share that status; otherwise cite claims individually without a misleading section label.
- Ancient text describes a tradition; it does not prove divine action.
- “Memory made architectural” is labeled as Aaron's reading.
- The name timeline must say “in this source,” not “the correct version is.”
- Any reconstructed plan must include a visible “interior partly uncertain” state.

## Story movement

### 1. The temple that will not become a rectangle

**Physical anchor:** the change of level and the four unlike façades.

- Establish the extant monument's 421–406 BCE date.
- Describe what a visitor can observe without reconstructing the interior.
- Introduce the Museum/YSMA explanation: uneven ground plus several cult requirements.
- Set up the reader's question: what had to remain here?

**Sources:** `ysma-erechtheion`; `acropolis-museum-erechtheion`.  
**Evidence:** mixed `artifact-documented` and institutional `scholarly-reconstruction`.

### 2. The Athena who belonged to the city

**Physical anchor:** the east porch and the absent wooden image behind it.

- Explain Polias as Athena “of/protectress of the city.”
- Distinguish her old xoanon from Athena Parthenos without previewing Chapter 4 in detail.
- Use the 409/8 BCE building account's “temple in which the ancient image is” as the closest material-era anchor.
- Present the heaven-sent image as an Athenian tradition, not an object biography.
- Briefly connect the image to the Panathenaic festival only if the final source set includes the Museum's Polias/Panathenaia records.

**Sources:** `acropolis-museum-athena-polias` **proposed**; `acropolis-museum-erechtheion`; `pausanias-1.26.6-7` **proposed**; `attic-inscriptions-ig-i3-474` **proposed**.  
**Evidence:** mixed.

### 3. In the lower room, a name with a hyphen

**Physical anchor:** the lower western room in the institutional plan.

- Name Poseidon-Erechtheus, Hephaistos, and Boutes.
- Slow down on the hyphen: Pausanias gives a Poseidon altar where Erechtheus also received sacrifice; Pseudo-Apollodorus gives a Poseidon-Erechtheus priesthood.
- State the safe conclusion: Poseidon and the local king were closely joined in cult.
- State the limit: no single source lets us replace every Erechtheus with Poseidon.

**Sources:** `acropolis-museum-erechtheion`; `pausanias-1.26.5`; `pseudo-apollodorus-3.15.1` **proposed**; `oxford-classical-dictionary-erechtheus-2018` **proposed**.  
**Evidence:** mixed `text-attested` and `scholarly-reconstruction`.

### 4. Two names that refuse a clean family tree

**Physical anchor:** a compact source timeline, not a genealogical tree.

- Homer: earth-born Erechtheus, fostered by Athena, placed in her sanctuary.
- Pseudo-Apollodorus: Erichthonius receives the earth-birth/Athena upbringing story; a later Erechtheus appears as Pandion's son.
- Explain that modern scholarship still debates whether these were once one figure or related doubles.
- Give the reader a practical rule: follow each source's name rather than forcing universal continuity.

**Sources:** `homer-iliad-2.546-551` **proposed**; `pseudo-apollodorus-3.14.6` **proposed**; `pseudo-apollodorus-3.14.8` **proposed**; `pseudo-apollodorus-3.15.1` **proposed**; both OCD entries **proposed**.  
**Evidence:** `text-attested` plus scholarly state-of-question.

### 5. The olive outside, the sea and trident in the rock

**Physical anchor:** three hotspots—olive/Pandroseion, salt water, trident outline.

- Reuse Chapter 1's careful distinction: Herodotus lists olive and “sea” jointly; Pausanias explicitly associates sea water and trident outline with Poseidon's claim.
- Connect the olive to the Pandrosium in Pseudo-Apollodorus and to Pandrosus' adjacent temple in Pausanias.
- Introduce the building account's Pandroseion-facing elements as an epigraphic anchor.
- Refuse the tempting continuity claims: today's tree is not proven to be the same individual; the ancient water and exact marks are not fully recoverable in their original state.

**Sources:** `herodotus-8.55`; `pausanias-1.26.5`; `pausanias-1.27.1-3` **proposed**; `pseudo-apollodorus-3.14.1`; `attic-inscriptions-ig-i3-474` **proposed**.  
**Evidence:** mixed.

### 6. The six women we call Caryatids

**Physical anchor:** one original Kore beside a diagram of the south porch.

- Begin with what is secure: six Korai supported the roof; garment folds and hair participate in structural design.
- Reveal that the site figures are copies, five originals are in Athens, and one remains in London.
- Explain that the building accounts call them Korai.
- Present interpretations as a carousel or stacked cards: punished women of Caryae (later Roman story, disputed), ritual dancers, Kekrops' daughters, Arrhephoroi/Panathenaic maidens, libation bearers over the Kekropion.
- End with the Museum's current preference for libation bearers, retaining the word “interpretation.”

**Sources:** `acropolis-museum-erechtheion-kore-a` **proposed**; `attic-inscriptions-ig-i3-474` **proposed**; `jutte-2022-contested-caryatids` **proposed**.  
**Evidence:** `artifact-documented` for the objects; `scholarly-reconstruction` for identity.

### 7. A building that remembers without explaining everything

**Physical anchor:** all four interaction layers visible together.

- Return to the asymmetry from the opening.
- Do not call the sanctuary harmonious, egalitarian, or reconciled.
- State Aaron's narrower judgment: the building preserves adjacency without producing one canonical story.
- Invite the reader to look again at the Caryatids—not as the answer to the monument, but as one visible part of a much denser archive.
- Link back to Chapter 1 for the contest and forward to Chapter 4 only when available for Athena Parthenos.

**Sources:** synthesis of prior sections.  
**Evidence:** explicit editorial judgment.

## Interaction plan

### Primary interaction: “What did this building have to keep?”

Use a simplified, accessible plan/axonometric illustration with four mutually composable layers:

1. **Building** — extant levels, east/north/south porches, main surviving walls.
2. **Cults** — Athena Polias; Poseidon-Erechtheus; Hephaistos; Boutes.
3. **Tokens and neighbors** — olive/Pandroseion; salt water; trident; Kekropion.
4. **Uncertainty** — lost interior, reconstructed partitions, disputed identities, text variants.

Each hotspot includes:

- preferred bilingual label and stable entity ID;
- evidence-state badge;
- one-sentence claim;
- stable source link;
- an “uncertain because…” line when relevant.

Keyboard and screen-reader users must be able to traverse the same layer order and hotspot content without spatial guessing.

### Secondary interaction: the two-name timeline

Three steps only:

- Homeric Erechtheus;
- Pseudo-Apollodoran Erichthonius and later Erechtheus;
- modern unresolved question.

Do not render a branching family tree that visually privileges one version as canon.

## Visual direction

- Preserve the series' limestone, aged pigment, muted blue-green, terracotta, and restrained gold vocabulary.
- Use an oblique architectural cutaway rather than a photoreal complete interior.
- Render documented stone sharply; reconstructed partitions as softer translucent planes; purely artistic people/light as plainly labeled atmosphere.
- The Caryatids should retain individual variation and structural weight without melodramatic strain.
- Do not depict them as chained captives or assign one mythic identity in the cover.
- Do not show the Athena Polias xoanon as a confidently detailed human statue. A veiled silhouette or empty cult-image locus is safer until visual research is complete.
- Do not show the present olive as a gigantic unbroken survivor from antiquity.
