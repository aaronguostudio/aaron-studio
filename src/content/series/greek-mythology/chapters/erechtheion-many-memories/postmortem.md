## What Worked in Production

- The article found a concrete disturbance without inventing a travel scene: a famous porch makes the monument recognizable, then the other façades undo that confidence.
- A physical walking route prevented the source set from becoming a catalogue.
- The hyphen gave Poseidon-Erechtheus a memorable explanation without pretending to solve the cultic relationship.
- The Herodotus chronology correction materially improved the evidence structure during red-team review.
- The Korai section held four boundaries at once—function, name, identity, and original/copy status—without losing narrative momentum.
- English and Chinese were edited as sibling editions. The claim sequence and source set match, while sentence rhythm is language-specific.
- Both automated language gates passed after the ending acquired a concrete viewing rule.

## Growth Lesson Applied and Rejected

- **Applied:** the live blog-growth context recommended a concrete bottleneck before an abstract framework. This chapter adapts that lesson as a visible contradiction—the building will not resolve into one symmetrical view—before naming “memory.”
- **Rejected intentionally:** the suggested `single-owner/shared-boundary` experiment belongs to AI workflow writing and was not transplanted into a mythology chapter.
- **Measurement caveat:** LinkedIn import gaps in the growth dataset have no bearing on this article because distribution work is outside the current scope.

## Lessons

- A chronology boundary can matter more than an extra source. The most dangerous sentence was not false on its face; it was a smooth transition that invited the reader to equate two different temples.
- Similar names need a reader payoff immediately after the source comparison. Otherwise accuracy feels like avoidable complexity.
- “We do not know” becomes compelling when paired with “we do know”: the Korai carried the roof; their precise represented identity remains open.
- Repeating the site/original distinction in the conclusion prevents a poetic synthesis from undoing a factual boundary established earlier.
- An editorial metaphor can survive a strong objection when its status is explicit. “Memory” remained useful only after terrain, source dates, and lost interiors were allowed to resist it.
- The mythology exception should remain explicit in future chapters: narrative knowledge does not need a product, AI, or organizational analogy to justify publication.
