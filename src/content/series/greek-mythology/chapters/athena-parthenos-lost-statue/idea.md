# Chapter Idea — Athena Parthenos, the Lost Goddess

## Raw topic

Reconstruct Pheidias's lost gold-and-ivory Athena Parthenos from four kinds of surviving witness: ancient description, public accounts, later reduced copies, and fragments that reproduce individual details such as the shield.

## Reader pain

Modern illustrations and the Nashville recreation make the statue easy to picture, but that apparent completeness hides the evidence problem. The original does not survive. No one later copy preserves every detail, and the ancient texts describe selected features rather than handing us a measured design file.

## Initial thesis

Athena Parthenos survives not in one faithful substitute but in a distributed archive. Texts, accounts, statuettes, and shield copies recover a strong silhouette while leaving the artistic presence of Pheidias's colossal figure irretrievably open.

## Why this chapter belongs now

The season has moved from fragmentary architectural sculpture to a missing sacred image once housed inside the Parthenon. Chapter 4 can make the reconstruction method itself into the story: not “here is exactly what she looked like,” but “here is how a lost image keeps generating new bodies.”

## Editorial exception

This remains `narrative-knowledge`. The financial evidence may reveal that the statue's removable gold also belonged to the city's emergency resources, but the chapter must not turn Athena into a modern balance-sheet metaphor or convert ancient talents into a sensational present-day dollar figure.
