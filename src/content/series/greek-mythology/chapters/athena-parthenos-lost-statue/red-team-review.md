# Red-Team Review — Athena Parthenos

## Issue 1 — The opening could imply entry into an inaccessible ancient interior

**Risk:** A first-person scene inside the naos might invent an experience from Aaron's family trip.

**Revision:** The article begins outside the Parthenon and explicitly asks the reader to imagine the inner room. Aaron's personal evidence is limited to the stated family visit and the felt absence.

## Issue 2 — “Lost” could invite a false destruction story

**Risk:** A dramatic transfer to Constantinople, named fire, or last sighting would be memorable but exceeds the completed late-antique research.

**Revision:** Refused a definitive ending and made the boundary itself part of the organizing question: each witness can return something, but none can narrate the original's final fate.

## Issue 3 — Pausanias could be treated as a workshop record

**Risk:** His detailed inventory may feel so complete that readers forget the description is centuries later than Pheidias.

**Revision:** Named Pausanias as a later ancient visitor and treated even the serpent's identification as his act of recognition, not an uncontested fifth-century label.

## Issue 4 — The removable gold could flatten the goddess into a bank

**Risk:** The most shareable fact could swallow the chapter and turn a cult image into a modern finance analogy.

**Revision:** Preserved both halves of Thucydides's statement—gold could be removed and had to be restored—and added the explicit boundary that the finance evidence changes our understanding of the material, not the statue's visual form. This is the substantive revision of the pass.

## Issue 5 — Later copies could be called exact replicas

**Risk:** The Varvakeion figure's completeness and Acr. 1362's institutional identification may encourage readers to treat Roman reductions as miniature photographs.

**Revision:** Used the National Archaeological Museum's cautious “gives us an idea” formulation, contrasted the copies' new scale and material, and separated repeated high-confidence features from face, surface, and exact proportion.

## Issue 6 — The shield's proposed portraits could become clickbait

**Risk:** Naming Pheidias and Pericles as certain figures would add a dramatic aside without the dedicated evidence needed to settle the identification.

**Revision:** Kept the existence of proposed portrait identifications source-marked, but left the names and verdict outside the chapter's argument.

## Issue 7 — “Distributed archive” could remain an elegant metaphor without mechanism

**Risk:** If all witnesses merely appear in a list, the thesis does not explain how partial evidence improves or limits reconstruction.

**Revision:** Each section now has a distinct job: words add attributes, accounts add material stakes, whole-figure copies add repeated contours, and the shield enlarges one detail. Their incompatibility becomes the correction mechanism.

## Issue 8 — Excessive caution could imply that nothing is known

**Risk:** The reader may finish with uncertainty but no usable picture of Athena.

**Revision:** The final section states the secure outline before naming open choices: a standing gold-and-ivory goddess with Nike, spear, shield, serpent, aegis, and helmet creatures is supportable; complete face, proportions, color, surface, lighting, and atmosphere are not fully recoverable.

## Substantive revision completed

The gold section now answers its strongest objection directly. It limits the fiscal evidence to the meaning and status of the material, preventing a striking fact from becoming the master explanation for the statue or a modern treasury metaphor.

## Decision

**PASS for prose polish.** The distributed-archive mechanism and evidence ceiling are stable; the remaining work is rhythm, naturalness, and bilingual alignment.
