# Canon Note — Athena Parthenos

## Canonical Idea

Athena Parthenos survives as a **distributed archive**. Ancient description, contemporary accounting, later whole-figure reductions, and copied details recover a reliable outline while preventing any single object or modern reconstruction from replacing Pheidias's lost original.

The canonical formulation is: **loss does not mean disappearance, and reconstruction does not mean recovery**.

## Reusable Frame

To examine a reconstruction of a lost work:

1. ask what an ancient observer described;
2. ask what contemporary records counted or paid for;
3. compare what later copies repeatedly preserve;
4. note where their scale, material, date, and details diverge;
5. label the choices supplied by the modern maker.

This is an evidence-assembly sequence for the mythology experience, not a digital-twin or business framework.

## Claims Added

- Athena Parthenos was a lost, approximately twelve-metre gold-and-ivory image inside the Parthenon.
- Pausanias preserves a strong ancient attribute inventory but remains a witness centuries later than the original's creation.
- EM 637 records public oversight, annual finances, and gold expenditure connected to the statue.
- Thucydides records Pericles describing forty talents of refined gold as removable in emergency and owed back.
- Varvakeion Athena and Acr. 1362 are useful Roman reductions, not exact miniatures of the original.
- The Strangford Shield is a later Roman copy of one component and retains paint traces; proposed portrait identifications remain outside the chapter's settled claims.
- “Distributed archive restores a silhouette, not the original encounter” is an editorial synthesis.

## Claims Updated

- A copy is both preservation and a new historical object; its later date and changed medium define the ceiling of its evidentiary value.
- The statue's gold can be described as sacred image material and recoverable civic wealth without reducing the goddess to a bank.
- “Lost” records present condition only. It does not authorize a definitive Constantinople, fire, or destruction story.
- Artistic interpretation includes exact face, proportions, supports, fine arrangement, color distribution, surface, light, and atmosphere.
