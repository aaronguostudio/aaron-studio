## Editorial Contract

- **Reader:** a curious traveler or museum visitor who has seen a complete Athena reconstruction and wants to know what can actually be recovered about the lost original.
- **Job:** assemble a reliable outline from distinct witnesses without treating any one copy as Pheidias's substitute.
- **Promise:** the reader will recognize the statue's secure attributes and understand why face, exact form, surface, color, and encounter remain partly new in every reconstruction.
- **Opening:** an imagined entry into the Parthenon's empty inner room and the missing approximately twelve-metre figure.
- **Contribution:** explain the lost statue as a distributed archive whose witnesses improve and constrain one another.
- **Boundary:** no definitive destruction tale, modern currency conversion, “statue as bank,” exact Roman replica, certain shield portraits, or complete recovered visual program.
- **Hypothesis:** “Athena survives everywhere except in her original body” should carry readers from wonder into evidence assembly; target signals are evidence-layer completion, copy comparison, source opens, deep scroll, and next-chapter navigation.

The active growth lesson was adapted into a concrete absence before the abstract phrase “distributed archive.” The unrelated business-frame experiment was rejected because the story's value lies in the statue's material and afterlife, not in an operator analogy.
