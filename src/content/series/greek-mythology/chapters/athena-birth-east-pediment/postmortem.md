The chapter should perform best when the page reveals the absence before revealing a complete Athena. The strongest reader-facing moment is expected to be the transition from “the birth is missing” to the discovery that the three familiar visual elements—Metis, cosmic armed emergence, and the axe—belong to distinguishable ancient witnesses.

- readers remember that the east pediment's center is less secure than its dawn frame;

- the evidence controls add trust without turning the chapter into a source catalogue;
- the final artistic layer feels more interesting, not less compelling, because its choices are visible;
- the chapter creates intent to continue through the Athens season.

## What Worked in Production

- The concrete-gap opening applied the active growth lesson without importing its AI/business frame.
- Reading inward from Helios and Selene gave this chapter a different spatial engine from Chapter 1.
- Three textual traditions were separated only at details that materially change the image.
- One disputed female figure demonstrated uncertainty without creating an encyclopedic cast list.
- Red-team revision changed the final interaction passage into a reader payoff.
- English and Chinese were edited as sibling narratives and retained an identical source set.

## Lessons

- Version boundaries should be designed before prose. Once a fluent composite story exists, separating its ingredients becomes harder.
- A monument's edge can be a stronger opening than its famous central subject when the center is what has disappeared.
- A single representative dispute teaches method better than a full list of disputed identifications.
- A disclaimer after a complete image is weaker than an interaction that makes the reader assemble evidence first.
- Poetic scale remains compatible with precision when the sentence before it names the inferential boundary.
- Source-registry adoption must precede integration; inline proposal IDs are not a substitute for shared registration.
