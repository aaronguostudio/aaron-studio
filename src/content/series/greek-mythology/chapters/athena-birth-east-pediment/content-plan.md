# Content Plan: Born at Dawn

## Voice check

**Positioning:** A curious visitor learning to read inward from surviving stone rather than using the monument to decorate a story already known.

**Voice rule:** Use first person only for the Acropolis encounter and final changed perception. Keep the evidence source-led. Avoid explaining every divine genealogy.

**Personal anchor:** Aaron's family trip and the paradox that the eye can be drawn most powerfully to the part of a monument that is gone.

## Hook

The birth of Athena occupied the east pediment's center. That is now the part we can see least clearly. What survives are the edges: the sun rising, the moon descending, and gods turned toward an absence.

## Core thesis

The east pediment staged Athena's birth as a cosmic interruption framed by dawn; its surviving witnesses tell us the scale of the event more securely than its exact choreography.

## Story movement

### 1. The missing birth

- Begin at the east/front pediment, not in Zeus's head.
- Pausanias names the subject; museum records locate Zeus and Athena at the lost center.
- Establish the four evidence states without listing them as a classroom framework.

### 2. Dawn survives at the edges

- Reveal Helios's hand and Selene's descending body.
- Explain the museum's dawn interpretation.
- Let the reader feel the common direction of the surviving frame toward the center.

### 3. Before the birth: Metis inside Zeus

- Use Hesiod for Metis, the succession prophecy, swallowing, and head-birth.
- Keep the violence and power dynamic visible without imposing a single modern psychological allegory.
- State plainly that Metis is not securely present in the surviving pediment.

### 4. When Olympus stops

- Use Homeric Hymn 28 for armed Athena, trembling Olympus, moving sea, and Helios halting his horses.
- Connect the hymn's solar image to the pediment as a resonance, not a proven source relationship.

### 5. Who swung the axe?

- Introduce Pindar's Hephaestus and bronze axe.
- Show the simple version boundary: Hesiod gives no axe here; Pindar does.
- Use one disputed statue identity to show why the complete cast also remains open.

### 6. Reconstruct the direction, not the certainty

- Return to the empty center.
- Explain what a generated image can responsibly complete: scale, spatial tension, dawn light, and a possible central action.
- Label precise face, gesture, cast, costume colour, metalwork, and instant as artistic interpretation.
- End on the insight that the witnesses still make the absent event present.
