# Chapter Idea — Athena's Birth on the East Pediment

## Raw topic

Enter Athena's birth through the surviving edges of the Parthenon east pediment: Helios rises, Selene sinks, divine witnesses frame the event, and the central figures of Zeus and Athena are gone.

## Reader pain

The familiar retelling often sounds certain: Zeus swallowed Metis, Hephaestus split his head, and Athena emerged fully armed. Ancient texts do preserve those elements—but not in one single account, and the surviving pediment does not preserve the central action well enough to certify every participant, pose, or gesture.

## Initial thesis

The east pediment made Athena's birth feel cosmic rather than private: dawn framed the event and the other gods became its witnesses. Its missing center now creates the chapter's essential tension—what survives around an event may tell us its scale without restoring its exact choreography.

## Why this chapter belongs now

Chapter 1 began with two central gods surviving only in fragments. Chapter 2 moves to an even sharper absence: the birth itself has vanished from the center, while the daybreak surrounding it remains legible. This advances the season's promise from “what survives?” to “how can the edge of a scene preserve its meaning?”
