## Editorial Contract

- **Reader:** A mythology reader or Athens traveler who pictures fifth-century tragedy inside today's visible stone theatre.
- **Job:** Separate site phases, festival institution, dramatic text, and modern staging while understanding what performance changed about myth.
- **Promise:** The reader can explain why the visible theatre is chronologically layered, how *Eumenides* makes one inherited conflict newly Athenian, and why the original performance cannot be recovered as a complete picture.
- **Opening:** The marble seats must be removed from the 458 BCE scene.
- **Contribution:** “Public argument” is defined as a performance condition—shared, competitive encounter—not a claim of universal attendance, direct participation, or democratic consensus.
- **Boundary:** No linear ritual-origin story, no universal claim that tragedy retold myth, no one-play theory of the genre, and no later architecture or object used as an unmarked premiere image.
- **Hypothesis:** A chronological visual reversal should create enough surprise to carry readers into the textual case. The primary channel is the season page; the primary 7-day signal is completion of all four phase states, supported by dwell on the *Eumenides* scene and season-hub return.
