# Red-Team Review — The Parthenon Frieze

## Issue 1 — “No caption” could collapse into relativism

**Risk:** A skeptical reader may hear “the stone does not name itself” as “the Panathenaic interpretation is only a guess.”

**Required revision:** State the weight of the leading reading in the opening, then explain its mechanism through the entire sequence rather than through authority alone.

**Revision completed:** The opening now calls the Greater Panathenaia the strongest and most widely accepted explanation. Part 3 shows how preparation, procession, offerings, sacrifice, convergence, gods, and cloth reinforce one another.

## Issue 2 — The alternative could become clickbait

**Risk:** Connelly's sacrifice reading is more dramatic than a cloth-handling dispute and could falsely appear to have equal consensus or to expose a museum error.

**Required revision:** Give the proposal enough detail to be intelligible, then compare explanatory scope and state the current weight directly.

**Revision completed:** Part 5 calls it a serious alternative, not a hidden caption, and adds the substantive distinction that the Panathenaic interpretation explains the complete program while the sacrifice reading begins by reidentifying the central family.

## Issue 3 — “Visible marble” could silently include reconstruction

**Risk:** A modern digital sequence includes early drawings and reassembled fragments; calling everything “visible” would obscure provenance.

**Required revision:** Distinguish extant forms, reconstructable lost arrangements, and modern eye-level display.

**Revision completed:** Parts 1 and 2 name the role of early drawings, dispersed collections, interrupted ancient viewing, and digital reassembly.

## Issue 4 — Plato could be made to caption the frieze

**Risk:** The peplos reference in *Euthyphro* may sound like direct proof that the central cloth is Athena's robe.

**Required revision:** Separate evidence for the festival's ritual world from evidence for this specific sculpture.

**Revision completed:** Part 3 states in the same paragraph that Plato mentions the robe and does not mention the frieze or identify its cloth.

## Issue 5 — The article could become an evidence-method lecture

**Risk:** Repeating “artifact, text, reconstruction, interpretation” would flatten the procession's speed, scale, and divine presence.

**Required revision:** Make observation a narrative act and reserve methodology language for the moments where confidence changes.

**Revision completed:** Parts 1, 2, and 6 use movement, scale, labor, and stillness to carry the reader. Evidence labels enter at the naming transitions, not as a detached checklist.

## Issue 6 — The authorial reading could become political overclaim

**Risk:** “A city approaching its gods” can drift into claims about democratic inclusion, civic propaganda, or representation of all Athenians.

**Required revision:** Mark the sentence as this chapter's visual reading and explicitly exclude larger political conclusions.

**Revision completed:** Part 6 distinguishes the bounded composition judgment from claims about every inhabitant, equality, or a complete ideology.

## Issue 7 — The visual can undo the prose

**Risk:** A seamless, full-color scene may feel more authoritative than the article's careful distinctions, especially if official photography is reused.

**Required revision:** Put chronology, evidence state, and rights boundary in the article's final visual handoff.

**Revision completed:** Part 7 separates ancient blocks, early drawings, modern assembly, scholarship, and artistic completion, and states that restricted archive imagery should not be copied.

## Issue 8 — Personal detail could be invented

**Risk:** A precise museum encounter or family dialogue would read vividly but exceed Aaron's supplied memory.

**Required revision:** Use only the family Greece trip and his stated fascination with the Parthenon sculptures.

**Revision completed:** The opening remains personal without supplying a gallery position, weather, dialogue, or encounter with a named block.

## Substantive revision completed

The first complete draft named the main interpretation's greater weight but did not explain that advantage sharply enough after presenting Connelly. The final draft now compares scope: the Panathenaic reading accounts for the complete processional program, while the sacrifice reading begins from a different central identification and asks the surrounding sequence to follow. This changes the argument, not only the wording.

## Decision

**PASS for prose polish.** The main interpretation, credible alternative, visual provenance, personal boundary, and authorial inference are all explicit. No additional research is required for the locked claim set.
