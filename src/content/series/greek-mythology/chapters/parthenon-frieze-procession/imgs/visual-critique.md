# Visual Critique — Procession Reading Band

## Decision

**PASS — accepted as `artistic-interpretation`.**

## What works

- The bands turn a difficult 160-metre program into a legible mobile reading path.
- Movement visibly slows from riders to offerings and then to the seated assembly and cloth.
- Solid stone, chipped seams, blank paper, and graphite continuation distinguish observation from completion.
- The image avoids generated text, literal sacrifice, Roman triumph, modern parade, battle, fantasy, and pristine seamless marble.

## Accepted caveats

- The requested 4:5 ratio was not returned; the accepted 1003 × 1568 portrait is intentionally recorded rather than silently described as 4:5.
- The gods, central group, pigment, and block sequence are more complete and orderly than the evidence permits. The image is a reading map, not a restoration.
- The forked colored paths can visually overstate parity between interpretations; UI copy must continue to call the Panathenaic reading leading and Connelly's proposal lower-consensus.
- Some role identities are made visually specific even though exact personal identification remains interpretive.

## Rejection triggers for a future version

Reject any replacement that prints “Panathenaia” into the marble, depicts a literal daughter sacrifice as established fact, presents both readings as equal answers, or erases fragment joins and missing blocks.
