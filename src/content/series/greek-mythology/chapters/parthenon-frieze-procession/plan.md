## Hook

Turn off every label. The horses still gather, the riders still move, sacrificial animals still approach, and larger seated figures still wait near the end. What disappears is not the procession, but our confidence about what to call it.

Within the opening, state the chapter's judgment: “the Panathenaic procession” is the strongest explanation of this movement, not a title preserved on the stone.

## Thesis

The Parthenon frieze is best understood as an evocation of the Greater Panathenaia, but that identity is an evidence-based interpretation rather than a carved label, and its enigmatic central cloth scene marks where a strong explanation must remain unfinished.

## Outline

### Part 1: Remove the name and keep the movement

- Open with concrete verbs and forms before any festival vocabulary.
- Establish the continuous Ionic band high around the cella: about 160 metres, 115 original blocks, carved about 443–438 BCE.
- Note that today's eye-level museum/digital sequence is a powerful modern reassembly, not the original segmented view through the colonnade.
- State the thesis inside the first 150 words.
- Evidence: F01–F04, F12.

### Part 2: What the marble can say by itself

- Move from preparation in the west through riders, chariots, carriers, vessels and animals toward the east.
- At the east, describe larger seated figures and the five-person woven-cloth scene without first naming every identity.
- Explain that lost areas sometimes rely on seventeenth- and eighteenth-century drawings; “visible today” and “reconstructable in the sequence” are not identical.
- Keep “official,” “hero,” “priest,” “peplos” and individual names out until their evidence enters.
- Evidence: F03, F04, F09.

### Part 3: Why Panathenaia is the best explanation

- Let the converging processional structure do the first piece of work.
- Add the festival world attested by Plato: the robe carried to the Acropolis at the Greater Panathenaia.
- Add the later *Athenian Constitution* account of procession, contests and provision of Athena's robe, with its fourth-century date visible.
- Explain the difference between corroborating a ritual context and captioning this specific relief.
- State current institutional and scholarly weight plainly: the Panathenaic reading is broad and strong, though its exact temporal form remains debated.
- Evidence: F05–F08.

### Part 4: The smallest action at the center of the whole work

- Slow the pace around the east-center five figures, folded cloth and two head-borne objects.
- Separate the visible action from the traditional terms “peplos handover,” “Archon Basileus,” and a fixed giver/receiver direction.
- Introduce the narrower possibilities inside the leading reading: delivery, reception, handling, or folding after a ritual phase.
- Use this scene to make the core distinction felt rather than merely explained.
- Evidence: F09, F11.

### Part 5: The other story the same cloth can carry

- Present Connelly's 1996 proposal briefly and accurately: the family of Erechtheus preparing a daughter for sacrifice within a mythic commemorative scene.
- Name what makes the reading attractive and what additional identifications it requires.
- Refuse a “choose your truth” poll and refuse a revelation headline.
- Return to the weight of evidence: serious alternative, not settled replacement or equal consensus.
- Evidence: F10, F11.

### Part 6: Mortals advance; gods remain still

- Return from the central dispute to the whole visual program.
- Contrast moving human and animal bodies with the larger, seated divine register.
- Offer Aaron's bounded reading: the work organizes a city's approach toward divine presence rather than merely cataloguing festival participants.
- Mark this as the article's judgment, not a surviving Athenian slogan or proof of democratic inclusion.
- Evidence: F03–F05, F15.

### Part 7: Put the label back—more carefully

- Replay the opening after the reader has seen the evidence chain.
- Restore “Greater Panathenaia” as the best working name while leaving the central cloth visibly open.
- End with a changed way of looking: uncertainty is not empty space around knowledge; it is the edge that keeps a good explanation honest.
- Let the CTA return to the season rather than pivoting to a business lesson.
