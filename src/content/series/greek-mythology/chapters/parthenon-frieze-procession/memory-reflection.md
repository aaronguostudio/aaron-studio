## Series continuity reviewed

Chapter 1 established four evidence states and the rule that a named ancient tradition is not the same as a verified event. Chapter 5 extends the same discipline from a damaged mythic scene to a largely continuous visual program whose subject has no surviving ancient commentary.

The change in emphasis matters:

- Chapter 1 asks how fragments, texts, and place preserve a contest.
- Chapter 5 asks how a coherent image can still resist a definitive caption.

## Ideas to reuse

- Begin with a contradiction the reader can see.
- Keep the evidence boundary inside the experience rather than in a distant methodology note.
- End with a changed way of looking at the object.
- Let one interaction carry the central argument.

## Continuity thesis

The series is teaching one cumulative practice: first ask what survives, then ask what story or interpretation allows the surviving evidence to become meaningful without pretending the gap has disappeared.
