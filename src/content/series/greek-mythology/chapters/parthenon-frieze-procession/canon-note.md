# Canon Note — Chapter 5

## Canonical Idea

**Best explanation is not the same as ancient caption.** The Greater Panathenaia is the most persuasive and broadly supported reading of the Parthenon frieze's whole program. Its status as interpretation should increase transparency, not flatten it into one equal option among many.

The central cloth scene establishes a second rule: confidence can vary inside one artwork. A strong overall identification can coexist with a genuinely open local action.

## Reusable Frame

For an ancient image without its original title:

1. describe forms and relationships before assigning names;
2. test which interpretation explains the greatest portion of the program;
3. separate ancient evidence for the surrounding ritual from evidence for the image itself;
4. pressure-test the leading reading at its least secure point;
5. label every visual completion by date and evidence type.

This remains a mythology-series reading practice, not a commercial or AI framework.

## Claims Added

- The frieze's Panathenaic identity is a leading institutional and scholarly reconstruction rather than a surviving ancient title.
- The east-center textile and five-person group are visible, while the direction and ritual phase of the action remain disputed.
- Connelly's Erechtheid-sacrifice reading is a serious named alternative whose truth is not established and whose consensus weight is lower.
- Modern eye-level continuity is produced through display and digital assembly; it is not identical to segmented ancient viewing.
- Artistic movement, complete faces, sound, atmosphere, and a fully restored crowd cannot inherit evidentiary authority from the stone.

## Claims Updated

- The series' artifact-first rule now covers a visually coherent object whose subject still depends on interpretation, not only fragmentary or lost works.
- “Uncertainty” now means calibrated confidence within an explanation, not a generic claim that myth has no answer.
- The season's relationship between humans and gods gains a compositional form: moving mortal effort approaches larger divine stillness. This remains Aaron's reading.
