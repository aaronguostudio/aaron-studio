# Video brief: Why I Share DHH's Enthusiasm for AI

## Target Audience
Product and engineering teams with differing levels of trust in AI.

## Desired Emotion
Grounded enthusiasm and willingness to try one shared task.

## Core Promise
Use Aaron's directly reported team trust gap to explain a proposed joint exercise. Show a specific task, success criteria, method transparency, a source/interpretation mistake, and a bounded keep-or-change decision.

## Title/Thumbnail Expectation
Why I Share DHH's Enthusiasm for AI. Thumbnail AI TRUST / AT WORK with approved cartoon portrait. Opening immediately identifies the team's different experiences; no Linux or migration hook.

## High-Shock Facts
- 5/5: Aaron reports different levels of AI trust in his company team; no invented employee quotes or motives.
- 4/5: DHH describes agents changing his working experience, not Aaron's project acceptance outcome.
- 4/5: A rewritten personal interpretation can become an unsupported general claim.
- 4/5: A successful writing task does not establish readiness for a shared business system.
- 3/5: Existing source/draft/review artifacts make the proposed exercise inspectable.
These are editorial stakes, not dramatic numbers or measured team results.

## Hook Type
Concrete team tension: individual confidence versus a method colleagues can inspect.

## Story Structure
Follow one prospective shared writing task: define good → show method → inspect one claim → let enthusiasm and skepticism contribute → bound the lesson → keep useful method. Does not narrate the blog section by section.

## What The Video Adds
- A proposed shared exercise with three explicit completion criteria, beyond the article's brief suggestion.
- A concrete two-sentence comparison with an unsupported rewrite illustrated as an example, not a real team failure.
- A keep/change decision and an explicit boundary on transferring trust to a more consequential task.
No interview footage/audio, no fictional team meeting, no shipped-tool claim.

The next discussion starts with a familiar task and agreed result; the team decides which method to keep. Return to useful products and the author's optimistic stance.
