# Video brief: Why I Share DHH's Enthusiasm for AI

## Target Audience
Product and engineering teams with differing levels of trust in AI.

## Desired Emotion
Grounded enthusiasm and willingness to try one shared task.

## Core Promise
Explain the three interview ideas that resonate with Aaron, then show what they mean in his existing blog workflow, directly reported team trust gap, and a proposed shared writing task. Target 7–8 minutes at the unchanged production voice speed. Interview highlights are concise paraphrases; Aaron's interpretation and operating choices carry most of the film.

## Title/Thumbnail Expectation
Why I Share DHH's Enthusiasm for AI. Thumbnail AI TRUST / AT WORK with approved cartoon portrait. Opening immediately identifies the team's different experiences; no Linux or migration hook.

## High-Shock Facts
- 5/5: Aaron reports different levels of AI trust in his company team; no invented employee quotes or motives.
- 4/5: DHH describes agents changing his working experience, not Aaron's project acceptance outcome.
- 4/5: A rewritten personal interpretation can become an unsupported general claim.
- 4/5: A successful writing task does not establish readiness for a shared business system.
- 3/5: Existing source/draft/review artifacts make the proposed exercise inspectable.
These are editorial stakes, not dramatic numbers or measured team results.

## Hook Type
Concrete team tension: individual confidence versus a method colleagues can inspect.

## Story Structure
Team tension cold open → three interview highlights (update judgments, product decisions, joy of creation) → actual correction of this blog's focus → team implications → one proposed task and a concrete claim error → delivery consequences → one useful method to keep. Retain the author's positive product stance. Omarchy is one short supporting mention.
