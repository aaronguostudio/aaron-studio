# Video brief: DHH and the Joy of Building With AI

## Target Audience
Product-minded developers and operators who already use AI and feel their list of possible projects expanding.

## Desired Emotion
Grounded excitement: the viewer wants to finish one personally useful thing.

## Core Promise
Take the excitement of DHH's interview and turn it into a small project decision using Aaron's real source-to-article workflow. This video is about choosing one useful experiment, told through a draft sentence and its supporting source, so the viewer can separate inspiration from delivery.

## Title/Thumbnail Expectation
Title: DHH and the Joy of Building With AI. Thumbnail: BUILD / WHAT YOU / CARE ABOUT. Opening begins with Aaron's real Linux curiosity; within 30 seconds it promises a way to select one project.

## High-Shock Facts
- 5/5: Aaron's actual reaction was wanting to try Linux, not choosing a new productivity metric.
- 4/5: A confident-looking paraphrase can outrun what an interview supports.
- 4/5: The source bundle here distinguishes sourced claims from Aaron's interpretation.
- 4/5: A functioning first version can be a useful stopping point.
- 4/5: Previous QA experience makes generated output and accepted change separate.
- 3/5: The proposed small tool could begin as a document and link, rather than a new application.
These are bounded lived observations/editorial examples, not sensational claims or performance statistics.

## Hook Type
Concrete personal reaction plus scope tension. Target: builders; transformation: many tempting ideas to one useful experiment; stakes: attention and maintenance.

## Story Structure
Follow one decision: inspiration → expensive start → real writing artifact → claim boundary → first-version scope → maintenance objection → usable stop rule. It does not mirror the article's headings.

The source interview does not contain Aaron's local documents or this proposed experiment. No interview footage or audio copied; attribution shown as source link.
