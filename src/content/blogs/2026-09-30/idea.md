# Idea

## Topic

**61% Said No. Why Did Greece Sign Anyway?** A close reading of Greece's 2015 referendum, the narrowing space for negotiation, and the distinction between a democratic mandate, legal scope, and the real ability to walk away.

## Why Now

Agents are being granted permissions faster than organizations are clarifying mandate, capability, approval, and stop conditions. "Allowed" and "able" are already being confused.

## Target Reader

An operator who delegates consequential decisions—through a team, a product workflow, or an AI agent—and needs a more precise vocabulary than autonomy versus control.

## Reader Pain / Curiosity

The referendum looks like an obvious betrayal or an obvious inevitability depending on the reader's prior politics. The useful question is harder: what did the vote authorize, and what options were actually viable?

## Initial Thesis

Authority gives a decision-maker legitimacy, not unlimited capability. A mandate without a credible alternative can change the terms of a negotiation without determining its outcome.

## Why This Should Exist

It preserves the emotional force of the 61.31% No vote while refusing the lazy ending that either the vote was meaningless or that it settled all later choices.

## Kill Criteria

Stop if the article claims the referendum rejected every future bailout, declares Tsipras's conduct unconstitutional without a decisive judicial ruling, or treats the September election as a collective ratification of every bailout term.
