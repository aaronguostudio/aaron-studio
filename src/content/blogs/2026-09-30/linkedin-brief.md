“Approved” and “possible” are not the same thing.

Greece's 2015 referendum makes that distinction unusually visible. 61.31% voted No to specific creditor documents. Days later, the government returned to negotiations and accepted a new assistance framework.

That does not mean the vote was meaningless. It changed legitimacy, negotiating posture, and the political cost of compromise. But it did not automatically create a safe path to every preferred outcome. Liquidity pressure, bank stress, and the real ability to walk away still shaped the decision.

Teams make the same mistake with delegation. They grant a person or an agent permission, then assume the action is safe, viable, and accountable.

For consequential work, I now separate mandate, permission, capability, constraint, and accountability.

Which of those five gets lost most often in your approval process?
