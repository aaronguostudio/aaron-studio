One of the questions that followed me home from Greece was this: how could 61% of voters say No, and the government still end up signing a new agreement days later?

The answer is not “the vote meant nothing.” It is more uncomfortable. A vote can give a government real authority while the available choices are still narrow, risky, and painful.

That feels relevant far beyond politics. We often say someone—or an AI system—is “approved” to act without asking whether the action is actually viable, safe, and owned.
