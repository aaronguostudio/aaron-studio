# LinkedIn Post

Most "AI agent" demos are still evaluated the way we evaluate a chatbot: by how good the output paragraph looks. That's the wrong interface, and it's why so many agent projects quietly rot — the plan drifts, facts slide into inference, and nobody notices until the smooth demo meets a real deadline.

I spent a week inside DeepSeek Harness running V4 Pro, and the useful signal wasn't the prose. It was the state: whether the plan was advancing, whether the claim ledger was separating fact from inference, and whether the agent had written down what "done" and "failed" meant before it started.

The model didn't get smarter. It got a body — tools it can program against, skills it loads as files, state it holds across turns, and a log you can audit. The operating rule that falls out of it: watch the state, not the prose.

Where have you seen a smooth demo hide a rotting plan?
