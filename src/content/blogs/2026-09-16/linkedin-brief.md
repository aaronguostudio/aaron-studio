I came home from a family trip to Greece with a financial question I could not leave alone: how could a country's reported debt look smaller without the underlying burden becoming smaller in the same way?

The Goldman/Greek swap story is often told as a tidy villain story. The more useful lesson is about metrics.

Debt/GDP was meant to stand in for fiscal sustainability. Once the number became a politically consequential target, the system had an incentive to improve the number itself.

We do this in product and AI work all the time. “Tickets closed,” “cost per task,” and “autonomous completion” are measurable. None guarantees the customer was helped or the system became safer.

My operating rule is simple: for every important metric, name the behaviour it encourages and the independent evidence that would prove the real outcome improved.

What metric has your organisation improved while making the underlying work worse?
