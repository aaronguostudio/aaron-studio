# Idea

## Topic

**The Debt Didn't Disappear. Greece Moved It Into the Future.** A family trip to Greece becomes a careful story about the 2000–01 Greek cross-currency swaps, fiscal statistics, and the danger of mistaking a reported metric for the economic reality behind it.

## Why Now

AI agents are increasingly evaluated by completion rate, cost, latency, or tickets closed. The Greek story is a vivid older example of the same incentive problem: a proxy becomes an eligibility target, and clever participants optimize the proxy.

## Target Reader

An operator, product leader, founder, or technically curious reader who uses metrics and is beginning to delegate work to AI agents.

## Reader Pain / Curiosity

The reader knows that KPIs can be gamed, but may not see how a seemingly technical classification decision can change incentives without changing the underlying obligation.

## Initial Thesis

When a number becomes the target, intelligence migrates toward making the number look better. The remedy is not to abandon measurement; it is to keep the metric connected to the reality it is supposed to stand for.

## Why This Should Exist

It starts from a real travel curiosity—what happened between Greece and Goldman Sachs—and ends with a usable rule for product metrics and agent evaluation. It is a financial story first, not a retrofitted AI explainer.

## Kill Criteria

Stop if the piece turns the swap into a false single-cause explanation for Greece's crisis, claims Goldman "got Greece into the euro," or cannot explain the accounting mechanism without pretending to be a technical accounting paper.
