Most shared systems look coherent when everything is working.

The harder test is simple: when a failure crosses boundaries, who can act, who bears the cost, and how does the system recover?

That is why I wrote about Greece's debt crisis as an architecture story. A shared currency gave Europe scale. But public debt, taxation, and democratic accountability remained largely national. When trust broke, bailout and non-bailout both allocated real pain.

The analogy to agents is structural, not moral. Before a production agent can write records, spend money, or deploy, its failure domain needs an owner, authority, containment, recovery, and a learning path.

Happy paths show performance. Failure modes show architecture.
