# Editorial Brief

## Reader Pain

Shared platforms look coherent from the happy path, but incidents reveal that the owners, recovery controls, and financial consequences are scattered.

## Reader Job To Be Done

Ask the right resilience question before a system is in distress: who owns the failure domain, who can act, and how does the system contain harm?

## One-Sentence Promise

By the end, the reader can use the Eurozone crisis to spot the missing recovery contract in a shared agent or product system.

## Sharp Thesis

A shared runtime without a clearly owned failure domain is not simply incomplete; it stores a political and operational decision until the worst possible moment.

## Concrete Opening

In 2009, Greece's fiscal deficit estimate was revised upward again and again, ending at 15.4% of GDP. The crucial number was not only large—it made refinancing depend on trust that had been damaged.

Name one scene, contradiction, bottleneck, result, or decision. Do not open with the topic in general.

## Original Contribution

The article reframes the crisis as a failure-handling architecture, while protecting the human stakes from an overly neat software analogy.

State what this article adds beyond the source material and Aaron's prior posts.

## Why Aaron Can Write This

Aaron designs systems where recovery, rollback, evidence, and bounded agent action are operational questions. The historical record is sourced; the architecture reading is explicitly an inference.

## Authority And Scope Boundary

It will not diagnose Greece's entire macroeconomic crisis, claim austerity alone caused the recession, or say the Eurosystem provided no lender-of-last-resort function.

State what Aaron knows directly, what is inferred, and what the article will not claim.

## Evidence Needed

- IMF/Eurostat deficit-revision record and programme history.
- IMF source for the €110bn 2010 programme.
- ECB sources for the Eurosystem and 2015 ELA ceiling.
- ESM/IMF source for the effects and limits of adjustment.

## Counterargument

Ambiguity can preserve flexibility during a crisis. But flexibility without named authority, funding, escalation, and recovery rules creates costly improvisation and moral hazard arguments under pressure.

## Reusable Frame

**Failure-domain contract:** trigger, owner, authority, funding/cost bearer, containment boundary, recovery path, and post-incident learning.

## Distribution Hook

“Happy paths show performance. Failure modes show architecture.”

## Success Hypothesis

The 2009 number creates a concrete first scene; the failure-domain contract makes the article useful for agent teams, applying the active concrete-bottleneck opening lesson.

## Kill Criteria

Redraft if the economic context becomes a mere prop for software language, or if the reader cannot distinguish liquidity stress, solvency risk, and the political choice of a bailout.
