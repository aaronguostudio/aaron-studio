# Astra: fewer corrections, a real finish line

## Target Audience
Builders trying Astra who have seen impressive demos but need to decide whether it improves their own work.

## Desired Emotion
Recognition followed by grounded excitement: this could relieve a familiar burden.

## Core Promise
Eight useful launch takeaways, illustrated through a reporting pipeline and a creative workflow; distinguish stronger execution from a finished result.

## Title/Thumbnail Expectation
Title candidate: GPT-6 Astra: I'm Correcting My AI Less.
Secondary option: GPT-6 Astra After the Demo: 8 Things to Know.
Thumbnail concept A: FEWER CORRECTIONS? beside one human hand and a completed work card.
Concept B: IT FINISHED? with a PDF arriving before an attachment check; explanatory illustration, no fake dashboard.
First 30 seconds name Astra, the personal observation, and the failed attachment that makes the observation worth qualifying.

## High-Shock Facts
- Real PDFs existed while the attachment step still failed: strong narrative proof of an incomplete chain.
- Aaron feels fewer corrections in engineering and writing; explicitly not a controlled benchmark.
- A WOW-titled Reddit post is a quota complaint: useful reveal about reading launch sentiment.
- Codex documents experimental notes/searchable history, off by default; active use in Aaron's run is unknown.
- Official guidance flags conflicting skills as a source of premature pauses.
- Better alignment coexists with reduced monitorability in OpenAI's adversarial evaluation.
- The strongest visual comparison is elapsed time versus active human attention, with no fabricated numbers.

## Hook Type
Specific failure and unexpected positive impression. Target: a builder. Transformation: from impressive output to accepted work. Stakes: wasted attention and unfinished delivery.

## Story Structure
Cold open at PDF/attachment seam → eight-point promise → the engineering chain (points 1–3) → correction and creative judgment (4–5) → cost and boundaries (6–7) → personal comparison card (8) → return to the finish line.

## What The Video Adds
- Progressive report-chain diagram exposes the precise point where output exists but delivery fails.
- Two independent clock faces separate waiting from active attention without invented timing data.
- Reveal of a misleading positive-sounding post title teaches viewers to inspect the report beneath it.
- A comparison card is filled one field at a time so viewers can reuse it on their next task.

## Banned Phrases
- No AGI verdict or universal superiority.
- No invented percentage gains or enabled-production claims.
- No "let's dive in", "game changer", "crazy", or "everyone agrees".

## Ending
Return to the report chain and the point where work can be accepted. The next task has a finish line; the upgrade is earning greater delegation with evidence and less correction.
