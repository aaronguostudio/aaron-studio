# Content Plan: AI Made Process Cheaper. Judgment Is Still Expensive.

## Voice Check

**Positioning:** Builder who inspected the machinery, not commentator reacting to AI headlines.

**Voice rule:** Use “I” for Aaron’s interpretation and research journey; use primary evidence for the large claims. Do not force a small personal anecdote into the role of proof.

**This post’s personal anchor:** Aaron spent days tracing a 12,293-commit agent-native repository. What changed his mind was not the amount of code, but the amount of organizational judgment that had been turned into gates, notes, tests, budgets, and recovery systems.

## Hook / Opening

**Blog hook:** OpenAI reports that an initially three-engineer team used Codex to produce roughly one million lines of code and 1,500 pull requests in five months, with no manually written code. The revealing choice was not removing programmers from the keyboard. It was introducing architecture constraints normally postponed until hundreds of engineers as an early prerequisite.

DeepSeek Harness independently arrived at the same inversion: when execution became cheap, structure moved earlier.

**X hook:** A 3-engineer team produced a million-line product with AI. Their response was not less process. It was architecture normally postponed until hundreds of engineers. AI did not remove process. It changed its economics.

## Core Argument / Thesis

AI converts good process from coordination overhead into organizational capital. When execution scales faster than headcount, advantage moves to the judgments a company can encode, reuse, and retire. The risk is symmetrical: AI also makes bad process easier to multiply.

## Outline

### Section 1: The Surprising Thing Was the Process

- Open with OpenAI’s three-engineer / one-million-line case using explicit self-report language.
- Contrast the output headline with the earlier architectural constraints, QA bottleneck, and eventual garbage-collection system.
- Introduce DeepSeek Harness as an independent convergence, not a second company profile.
- State the thesis within the first 400 words: the cost curve of process changed.

### Section 2: A Harness Is Organizational Capital

- Introduce the productivity J-curve: general-purpose technology needs complementary investment in process, products, business models, and human capital.
- Define a harness as that idea made operational at repository scale: model-visible context, enforceable constraints, feedback loops, evaluations, and memory.
- Explain why this is more useful than treating a harness as prompt engineering or a collection of tools.
- Aaron beat: the DeepSeek teardown looked less like a clever coding setup and more like an organization encoded into a filesystem.

### Section 3: Two Agent-Native Systems Found the Same Boundary

- OpenAI: enforce invariants centrally, allow autonomy locally; promote repeated review feedback into code.
- DeepSeek: 683 notes, rejected decisions, postmortem-to-verifier pattern, translation and documentation contracts.
- Do not celebrate density. Show the shared logic: a decision made once can be applied across thousands of agent actions.
- Name the compounding loop: judgment → encoding → cheap execution → new evidence → refined judgment.

### Section 4: The Work Did Not Disappear. It Moved Upward.

- Use Anthropic’s roughly 400,000 sessions: humans make about 70% of planning decisions and 20% of execution decisions.
- Domain expertise produces more successful sessions and more agent work per instruction.
- Connect to Aaron’s existing one-person-project thesis: ownership shifts toward defining the problem and acceptance boundary.
- Use DORA’s “amplifier” conclusion: agents multiply the organizational system already present.

### Section 5: Cheap Process Can Become Expensive Clutter

- DeepSeek’s 178 green tests / 100% coverage still missed the real loader path.
- OpenAI’s weekly 20% “AI slop” cleanup shows that encoded patterns can amplify drift.
- Explain process inflation: rules become easy to create, so comprehension, contradictions, and retirement become the new tax.
- Take the strongest objection seriously: most small teams should not copy AI-lab process volume.

### Section 6: The Process Capital Test

- Present three questions:
  1. Does this artifact capture a recurring, expensive judgment?
  2. Can an agent or machine apply/check it without continuous human interpretation?
  3. Who owns its revision or retirement?
- Failures: bureaucracy, hidden toil, institutional fossil.
- Give compact examples at three scales: acceptance test, architecture invariant, rejected decision with expiry/owner.
- The practical action is subtraction as often as addition.

### Section 7: The New Management Surface

- Return to the title: process became cheaper; judgment did not.
- Competitive advantage comes from deciding which judgment should compound through the harness.
- End with a forward-looking claim: the AI-native organization will be distinguished less by how many agents it runs than by how quickly it converts experience into better constraints—and removes constraints that no longer earn their keep.
