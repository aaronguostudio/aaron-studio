# Blog Plan: AI Made Process Cheaper. Judgment Is Still Expensive.

## Meta

- **Author:** Aaron Guo
- **Target:** aaronguo.com blog (EN + ZH bilingual)
- **Tone:** Entrepreneurial, crisp, insight-led, commercially useful; evidence-aware rather than breathless
- **Length:** 2,300–2,700 words
- **Audience:** Product and engineering leaders, founders, and AI-native builders
- **CTA:** Newsletter

## Hook

OpenAI reports that an initially three-engineer team drove Codex to produce roughly one million lines of code and 1,500 pull requests in five months. The detail that matters is not that humans stopped typing code. It is that the team adopted architectural constraints normally delayed until hundreds of engineers as an early prerequisite.

DeepSeek Harness, built in a different organization, converged on the same economic inversion.

## Thesis

AI converts good process from coordination overhead into organizational capital; as execution becomes abundant, advantage moves to the quality of judgments an organization can encode, reuse, and retire.

## Personal Anchor

Aaron’s primary-source teardown of 64 days and 12,293 commits in DeepSeek Harness. The operator insight is that the repository is not merely code plus agent prompts; it is an organization whose memory, constraints, and recovery behavior have been partially compiled into the environment.

## Outline

### Part 1: The Headline Was Code. The Real Story Was Structure.

- OpenAI’s reported scale and team shape.
- Strong constraints moved earlier rather than disappearing.
- DeepSeek’s independent convergence.
- Land the title and thesis quickly.

### Part 2: Process Changed Economic Categories

- Productivity J-curve and complementary intangible capital.
- Define harness as organizational capital in executable form.
- Explain why process once felt like overhead: it consumed the same scarce human labor it coordinated.
- Explain why the new cost curve is different: agents can author and repeatedly execute the mechanism.

### Part 3: Judgment Can Compound

- OpenAI: invariants, feedback promotion, repository legibility.
- DeepSeek: rejected reasoning, postmortem-to-verifier, machine-visible operational facts.
- One human judgment, applied across thousands of future actions.
- Name the compounding loop.

### Part 4: Judgment Did Not Disappear

- Anthropic’s 70% planning / 20% execution result.
- Expertise as leverage over agent output.
- DORA’s amplifier conclusion.
- What, why, acceptable, and exceptional remain the human layer—for now.

### Part 5: Every New Capital Stock Creates Maintenance

- OpenAI’s 20% weekly slop cleanup before automation.
- DeepSeek’s real-loader failure behind 178 green tests.
- Gate forests, stale decisions, and false confidence.
- Strong concession: ordinary teams should not copy lab-scale density.

### Part 6: The Process Capital Test

- Recurring judgment.
- Executable application.
- Depreciation owner.
- Use compact examples and include a deletion example.

### Part 7: The New Management Surface

- Process is cheaper; judgment is still expensive.
- The strategic capability is deciding which judgment should become infrastructure.
- End on compounding and retirement, not on agent hype.
