# Final Video Direction: Authority, Not Implementation

## Why This Revision Exists

Aaron approved the porcelain-and-indigo typographic direction but rejected the
prototype's remaining report chrome, ruled background, abrupt 13–14 second page
cut, and implementation-level customer-status example. The new prototype tests
the argument at concept level before any full-film work continues.

## Viewer Promise

In under one minute, replace “Should I read AI-generated code?” with the more
useful question: “What is this code allowed to change?”

## Locked Content Boundary

- No status-field example.
- No line-level implementation or test walkthrough.
- No downstream billing, access, email, or reporting inventory.
- No review taxonomy explanation beyond one concise operating rule.
- Keep `critical system` and `system of record`, because they are Aaron's lived
  boundary and the point of the article.

## Selected Visual Direction — Clean Indigo Editorial

- Pure warm porcelain canvas; no grid, paper texture, horizontal ruling, glow,
  gradient, dashboard surface, or card wall.
- Header reads only `AARON GUO`; `FIELD JOURNAL` is removed.
- Deep indigo remains the sole accent. Dark indigo may own one short emphasis
  beat, but it is not the persistent theme.
- Each scene carries one sentence-sized idea and one dominant reading axis.
- Captions remain quiet and phrase-level in the protected lower zone.

## Five Beats

1. **Wrong question** — `READ THE CODE` and `TRUST THE EVIDENCE` appear as two
   legitimate positions, then yield to a larger question.
2. **Low-risk lane** — disposable, reversible work can move quickly.
3. **Authority boundary** — a single axis moves from `DISPOSABLE` to
   `AUTHORITATIVE`; `CRITICAL SYSTEM` and `SYSTEM OF RECORD` cross the boundary.
4. **Operating rule** — review depth rises with authority; only three human
   responsibilities remain: intent, risk, recovery.
5. **Payoff** — `AI CAN PROPOSE` resolves into `A HUMAN OWNS THE RESULT`.

## Transition Contract

The 13–14 second change is not a page cut. The center divider from the opening
split becomes the authority axis of the next scene while the old words clear.
Only one information hierarchy owns each frame; no two dense pages crossfade.

## Motion Budget

- Calm: 55% — statements and holds.
- Structured: 40% — shared-axis transition and authority scale.
- Signature: 5% — the final ownership inversion.
- Meaningful change every 4–8 seconds; no motion merely to decorate a screen.

## Production Decision

Aaron approved the shorter argument, clean field, continuity transition,
pacing, and single-use ledger accent. This direction is the final film; the
older detailed five-minute treatment remains superseded.
