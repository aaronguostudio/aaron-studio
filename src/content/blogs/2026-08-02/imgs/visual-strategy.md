# Visual Strategy

## Article Character

Primary: **Technical framework / know-how**

Secondary: opinionated operator essay.

The article begins with a live engineering disagreement, then turns it into a risk-allocation rule for critical systems and systems of record.

## Reader Emotion

The set should move the reader from curiosity to alertness to operational clarity:

- curious about the Mitchell / Uncle Bob contradiction;
- slightly uneasy about small changes gaining large authority;
- confident that the review decision can be made deliberately.

It should feel serious without becoming fearful, compliance-heavy, or corporate.

## Visual Risk

- Generic glowing code, robots, brains, or cyber-security imagery.
- A literal split-screen portrait of Mitchell and Uncle Bob that turns the article into personality commentary.
- Too many diagrams for a roughly 900-word essay.
- A dense system-of-record architecture that makes the simple thesis harder to grasp.
- Tiny fake code or unreadable labels.
- Repeating the cyan-glass ribbon language from prior posts.
- Making the cover look like a legal or accounting article rather than an AI-native engineering argument.

## Argument Beats That Need Images

| Image | Article beat | What it makes visible | What is lost without it | Proposed position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 00-cover | Code does not have equal authority everywhere | A small generated change approaching the boundary where it becomes operational fact | The title remains abstract and risks looking like another generic “read AI code?” debate | Blog header |
| 01-authority-ripple | Reverting code may not restore records or downstream decisions | One small authoritative write propagating into several trusted outcomes | The difference between code rollback and restoring reality is harder to feel | After the paragraph ending “decisions already made” |
| 02-review-depth-ladder | Outcome → Evidence → System Model → Critical Path | Human attention deepens as a change moves from disposable to authoritative | The four review levels are useful but less memorable and shareable | Immediately after the four-level list |

Only two body images are recommended. A third body image would over-illustrate this short article.

## Concept Routes

| Route | Central predicate | Composition | Best role | Why it fits | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Authority Gate | A provisional code strip becomes an authoritative record only after crossing a controlled boundary | Wide horizontal editorial composition: a human hand guides a narrow graphite code strip toward a muted-coral gate; beyond it, the strip becomes an ordered ledger surface with a few downstream paper forms | Cover and thumbnail world | Directly visualizes “what is this code allowed to change?” without a robot or dashboard | The gate could feel like generic security unless the record transformation is obvious |
| B — Small Key, Large Ledger | A tiny change can unlock a much larger field of consequences | Close human-scale composition: a small code-shaped key held above a large mechanical record book or vault-like ledger, with restrained downstream traces | Cover or body metaphor | Makes the “five lines can matter more than 1,000” point tactile and memorable | Key / lock imagery can become familiar or overly dramatic |
| C — State Ripple | One wrong authoritative value continues beyond the code that created it | Calm cutaway map: one muted-coral mark enters a central record, then propagates to four large downstream artifacts; a rollback arrow stops before those artifacts | Mechanism image and framework-adjacent cover | Best explanation of rollback code versus restore reality | Can become a diagram wall and reveal too much of the article on the cover |

## Selected Concept Route

**Aaron-confirmed: Route A — Authority Gate.**

Route A is the clearest cover-level expression of the title, has a strong left-to-right predicate, and can support a thumbnail without forcing dense labels. Aaron confirmed the recommended route and controlled-mix treatment on 2026-08-02. Route C is retained for the first body image, where explanatory detail is useful.

## Recommended Mode Mix

- Cover: **Editorial Minimal**, loud.
- Thumbnail: **Editorial Minimal + typography**, loud.
- Authority-ripple body image: **Human-Scale Metaphor** with a light **Cutaway System Map** influence, medium.
- Review ladder: **Operator Diagram**, medium.
- Glass / Fluent Artifact: no more than a 10–15% accent in the ladder; not a set-wide treatment.

## Cover Direction

A wide, tactile editorial image with three major elements:

1. a human hand and narrow graphite code strip on the left;
2. a single muted-coral authority gate at center;
3. a warm-white ordered ledger plane on the right, with two or three quiet downstream paper artifacts.

The code is still provisional before the gate. It becomes trusted state after the gate. No text, logos, screens, or literal database cylinders.

Thumbnail headline:

- Eyebrow: **AI WROTE IT**
- Body: **WHAT CAN IT**
- Hero: **CHANGE?**

## Body Image Direction

Use only two body images:

- one tactile metaphor that lets the reader see an authoritative state ripple after code rollback;
- one clean, reusable diagram of the four review depths.

Keep large shapes, high negative space, and one predicate per image. The body set should be quieter than the cover.

## Framework / Diagram Direction

The ladder should use four large horizontal depth bands or steps, moving from a loose paper output to a protected record core:

`Outcome → Evidence → System Model → Critical Path`

Risk moves left-to-right from disposable / reversible to authoritative / hard to restore. Exact text is limited to the four level names and two axis-end phrases. No icons inside every level, no nested arrows, and no explanatory paragraphs inside the image.

## What To Avoid

- Portraits or caricatures of Mitchell Hashimoto and Uncle Bob.
- Binary “human versus AI” split imagery.
- Code rain, robot hands, glowing brains, database cylinders, sci-fi HUDs.
- More than three major cover elements.
- More than five major framework elements.
- Fake code, fake dashboards, decorative arrows, and microcopy.
- Heavy dark-blue palettes or repeated glowing cyan routes.
- A third decorative body image with no distinct explanatory job.
