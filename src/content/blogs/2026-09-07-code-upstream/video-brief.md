# Video brief — Build what matters

## Target Audience
Engineers wondering how to invest their next five to ten years as AI handles more implementation.

## Core Promise
Turn cheaper execution into stronger business and product judgment through five concrete career choices.

## Hook Type
A judgment Aaron reached while working at Amazon: the ability to fund many jobs does not establish that the business needs a team of that size. Later restructuring did not surprise him. Preserve this as personal judgment, distinguish public facts, and avoid assigning worth to laid-off individuals.

## Story Structure
An old judgment becomes newly relevant → move engineering attention upstream → follow a hypothetical document tool from customer observation through design choice, adoption cost and actual use → show how one project develops longer-term capability → a hopeful personal ambition.

## What The Video Adds
- Place organizational capacity and customer needs in two visual lanes, with dated corporate statements distinctly attributed.
- Show two alternative document-review approaches, then the question a real-user comparison answers; this is hypothetical, not a product demo.
- Carry the same small product from problem discovery through adoption and follow-up, making cumulative capability visible in a closing path.

## Evidence
Use the existing nine-source research pack and claim ledger. Spoken references: Amazon dated statements, Boris Cherny's personal practice, Simon Willison's alternatives/usability advice, Kent Beck's payroll example, Microsoft Digital's self-report. No fake documentary screenshot or source endorsement.

## Pacing
About eight minutes using the existing approved Aaron production voice, 110–115 spoken wpm planning. Final durations follow the generated audio. Visual beats about 15–30 seconds, small structured focus shifts where useful, three image-led pauses. No forced gaps in mid-sentence. Chaptered quiet music; narration gain fixed.

## Ending
Return to Aaron's original judgment, then his choice for the next decade: learn what people need and bring worthwhile products into use. Finish on a grounded personal ambition with room to settle, followed by the established quiet brand card. No spoken subscription CTA.

## Banned Phrases
- The hypothetical example stays labeled. Aaron's small report project stays one sentence, development environment only. The career horizon is a direction to invest in, not a forecast of guaranteed jobs or a claim that engineering quality no longer matters.

## Desired Emotion
Purpose, curiosity, grounded ambition. No manufactured fear about layoffs.

## Title/Thumbnail Expectation
AI Can Write More Code. What Should Engineers Learn Next? / BUILD WHAT MATTERS. The opening connects a personal organizational judgment to a career choice.

## High-Shock Facts
- Cherny describes AI writing his code with continued human review: a bounded personal practice, not a universal result.
- Microsoft Digital reports faster individual development initially did not improve team productivity: an organizational self-report, not a cross-industry statistic.

## Retention Beat Map
- Opening: an old judgment and a current choice.
- Dated corporate statements, then their limited meaning.
- Watch a customer pause: the apparent problem changes.
- Compare two approaches: a prototype serves a question.
- Adoption costs change the commercial calculation.
- Completion requires follow-through.
- Return to the next decade and Aaron's personal ambition.
