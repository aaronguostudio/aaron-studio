## Hook / Opening

**Blog hook:** We were close to boarding when a missing guardianship-related document stopped one person in our party. We did not ask GPT to overrule the carrier; we asked it a narrower question: among the residency and immigration materials we already had, what might count as relevant proof? That question changed the speed of the search, not who had the authority to decide.

**X post hook:** “On my Greek holiday, GPT helped us search for a boarding-document clue, I shipped a large DrumNext upgrade from shipboard Starlink, and I never opened Google Search. This is what AI looked like after the itinerary ended.”

## Core Argument / Thesis

I did not take an “AI-planned trip.” I took a trip in which AI had become the fastest way back into the context I needed—documents, local questions, a codebase, a new framework, and a draft—when real life stopped following a plan.

The important boundary is as practical as the story: AI can help find, translate, compare, and structure; people and official sources still own consequential decisions, product judgment, and what a holiday is for.

## Narrative Spine

```text
boarding friction → live search for context → days in Greece → shipboard product work
→ evening team overlap → framework learning and writing → one quiet change in habit
```

The article should feel like a record of one trip, not a sequence of product demos. Every technical aside must return to an observable scene.

## Outline

### 1. The boarding problem was not in the itinerary

- Start immediately at the embarkation point, with time pressure and incomplete paperwork.
- State the narrowly useful GPT role: help locate possible relevant proof in documents already held.
- State the boundary in the same breath: it did not decide eligibility; carrier staff and official rules did.
- Do not name the companion, daughter, carrier, exact document, exact outcome, or immigration status.

### 2. Greece stayed a holiday

- Travel through cities and islands; use GPT for history/classics, restaurant decisions, translation and respectful bargaining/merchant communication.
- Keep sensory detail from Aaron’s own notes if supplied later; do not invent sunsets, island names, meals, or conversations.
- Make the Google Maps / no Google Search observation here, as a quiet fact rather than a victory lap.
- Explain that Maps still solved the spatial job; GPT condensed the research-and-language job.

### 3. A ship cabin became a small product studio

- Scene: returning to the room at night, opening DrumNext over the ship’s Starlink connection.
- Give the product reader a compact but real receipt: this release refreshed the practice experience with custom practice/setup flows, internal timing and random-mute training, speed work, presets, practice tracking and weekly goals, with persistence/sync and test coverage.
- Do not list 194 files or 11k lines in the main narrative unless a footnote/caption needs the receipt; the user-facing change matters more.
- Explain why DrumNext belongs in the story: AI helps re-enter a complex product context quickly enough that small windows can still lead to a coherent upgrade.
- Optional internal link: **The One-Person Project**.

### 4. Europe gave the day two different clocks

- Daytime: travel, cities, meals, presence. Night: overlap with the team.
- Describe the logistical fit, not a moral about permanent availability.
- Acknowledge the risk: a working holiday can become a bad bargain if work is allowed to expand without a reason; Aaron chose specific windows and returned to the trip.

### 5. From a new framework to a written memory

- Briefly introduce the DeepSeek Harness work as a technical thread Aaron was learning, not as a separate deep dive.
- GPT helped form a map of a new codebase/framework; Claude Code helped turn engineering understanding into working changes; the blog workflow helped turn raw experience into a useful story.
- Do not claim authorship of DeepSeek Harness or make comparative performance claims about models.
- Optional internal link: **Why I Stopped Writing Blog Posts (And Built an AI Studio Instead)**.

### 6. I noticed the missing browser tab only after the trip

- Return to the observation: Google Maps was open, but Google Search was not.
- Do not say search is obsolete. Say the sequence changed: instead of query → tabs → snippets → reassembly, many small questions began as a conversation with enough personal and situational context to propose the next move.
- Close on the human decision: AI did not make the trip meaningful. It made the friction lighter when it mattered, leaving more room for the actual trip.
- Optional internal link: **I Gave Codex a Task From a Moving Tesla**.
