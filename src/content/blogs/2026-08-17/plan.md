# Blog Plan: A Greek Trip I Couldn't Have Taken the Same Way Without AI

## Meta

- **Author:** Aaron Guo
- **Target:** aaronguo.com blog (EN + ZH bilingual)
- **Tone:** Grounded, personal, scene-led; clear operator detail when DrumNext appears; no theory-first framing, self-help, or travel-guide voice.
- **Length:** 1,800–2,200 words (English); Chinese sibling is an adaptation, not a line-by-line translation.
- **Audience:** AI-native builders, product operators, and independent creators who want a more realistic picture of AI in life and work.
- **CTA:** Newsletter — invite readers to subscribe for more field notes from building with AI.

## Hook

We were almost on the boat when an incomplete guardianship-related document stopped one person in our party. The itinerary had no answer for that. I opened GPT and asked the limited question that was actually useful: among the residency and immigration materials already on our phones, what might be relevant proof? It helped us search. It did not decide whether any document was acceptable.

Within roughly 150 words, name the thesis: this was not an AI-planned trip; it was a trip in which AI became the fastest way back to the context I needed after reality changed the plan.

## Thesis

AI became essential to this Greek holiday not because it planned everything in advance, but because it restored context—documents, language, code, and writing—when the trip kept producing questions a plan could not anticipate.

## Personal Anchor

A direct travel experience in Greece that included a time-sensitive boarding-document search, daily GPT use for local questions, shipboard Starlink work on a real DrumNext practice-experience upgrade, European evening overlap with the team, DeepSeek Harness learning, and the creation of this blog package.
