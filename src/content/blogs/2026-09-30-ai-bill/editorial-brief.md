# Editorial Brief

## Author Intent

Aaron's own words in this session: "这个对比，非常值得...好好对比一下这次 Open AI 的政策变化，对比解读，社区反应...最后加上我对未来 AI 收费的趋势的预测和对程序员的建议." He asked which plan is the better deal and accepted the recommendation to anchor everything on one thesis: **AI 订阅正在变成云账单**. His attitude is analytical, not outraged: he pays for both, wants the numbers, and wants a forward view programmers can act on. Peripheral: Dots, ChatGPT Space, DevDay product list (one sentence at most).

## Reader And Promise

Developers and technical leads paying for Codex / Claude Code plans. Promise: understand what actually changed (the unit, not just the quota), see one heavy user's real bill priced at list, know what to do before 10-29, and leave with a way to compare any AI plan.

## Article Form And Lead Language

Real-work case + sourced explainer, opinion-led at the end. Draft English first (video is English for the ai-native-builder channel); Chinese is a sibling edition, adapted not translated.

## Material And Opening

Open on the concrete disturbance: the OpenAI email (Sept 29) and Tibo's line "half the dollar in API spend." Aaron is a subscriber; his Codex meter hit 99-100% five times in September. Then the question: what am I actually getting for $200?

## Authority And Evidence Boundary

- Facts: plan terms (email, Tibo, TNW), API prices (official pages).
- Personal observation: priced logs from one Mac, 30 days; Codex numbers are a lower bound; a single heavy user, not a benchmark.
- Inference: linear pricing ($20 per 1x); cache-read price as the real price of agentic coding.
- Judgment/forecast: where pricing goes next; labeled as Aaron's inference.
- Unknown: Plus allowance in absolute dollars; Claude Max token allowance; whether 6.1 Sol can do his Astra work (planned test, not a result).

## Structure And Landing

1. The email and the unit (API dollars). 2. The bulk discount disappeared. 3. What the community heard (short). 4. My bill at list price. 5. The re-read is the real price. 6. Which is cheaper, and what I'm doing before 10-29. 7. What I expect next (forecast). 8. What programmers should do. Landing: in August I wrote the subscription paid the meter for me; now it tells me the exchange rate. That's not a betrayal, it's a bill I can finally read, and reading it is now a core engineering skill.

## Distribution Hypothesis

Discovery via X/LinkedIn developer audiences searching "Pro 200 halved" this week; the receipt-based angle ("I priced my own bill") matches the top-performing pattern (insider test on real work). Measure: CTR on YouTube title with dollar figures; blog read depth to the cache-read section.
