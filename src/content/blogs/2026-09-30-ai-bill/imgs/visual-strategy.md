# Visual Strategy

## Article Character

Personal AI workflow essay with an evidence-led, receipt-style argument (one heavy user's priced logs). Secondary character: market argument about subscriptions becoming cloud bills.

## Reader Emotion

Recognition, then clarity: "that's my bill too", followed by calm, numerate confidence. Not outrage, not hype.

## Visual Risk

- Generic glowing-AI or vendor-logo imagery.
- Fake receipt text or fake numbers in generated images (the article is about real numbers; invented ones would undermine it).
- Chart walls: five data charts is a lot for one post.
- Stock-photo "money/finance" clichés (coins, credit cards, piggy banks).

## Argument Beats That Need Images

| Beat | Image | What the reader grasps faster | Lost if removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title / thesis | 00-cover | A bill where one line item dwarfs the rest | The scroll-stopping "line item that matters" idea |
| The bulk discount is gone | 01-price-per-unit | Only one tier was ever below $20 per 1× | The flat-rate shape is harder to see in a table |
| My bill at list price | 02-codex-meter | The meter, not money, was the constraint | "Five of six windows" stays abstract |
| The real price is the re-read | 03-bill-by-model | Cache reads are most of every big line | The central evidence for the thesis |
| Cache-read price table | 04-cache-read-price | The 5× gap on the line that dominates | Readers skim past the middle column |
| Which plan is the better deal | 05-model-vs-vendor | Model choice moves the bill more than vendor choice | The repricing lever stays a sentence |

## Concept Routes

| Route | Central predicate | Composition | Best role | Why it fits | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | One line item swallows the whole bill | Paper receipt of identical graphite rows; one amber row overflows the receipt | Cover + thumbnail | Directly states "price your own bill; find the line item that dominates"; amber rhymes with the cache-read bars in the charts | Fake receipt text; amber reading as tape |
| B | A flat pass becomes a metered utility | Blank subscription card inserted into an analog electricity meter, needles pinned | Cover | Nails "subscriptions are becoming cloud bills" | Card reads as a bank card; less about the re-read; busier object |
| C | The same context is read again and again | Tower of identical pages with an amber loop feeding top back to bottom, mechanical counter beside | Cover | Most literal about the re-read mechanism | Needs explanation; loop reads as decoration; weak at thumbnail size |

Probe renders of all three are in `candidates/00-cover-probe-{a,b,c}-*.png`.

## Selected Concept Route

Agent-selected (decision delegated in the production brief): **Route A, receipt with one overflowing line item.** It is the only route that reads in under a second at 320px, it maps one-to-one onto the article's method (price the bill, find the dominant line), and its amber bar is the same visual token the charts use for cache reads, so cover, thumbnail and data slides feel like one system. B was the runner-up (strong metaphor for the first half of the thesis, but not the re-read). C was most literal but least legible.

## Recommended Mode Mix

- Editorial Minimal: cover and thumbnail.
- Operator Diagram (deterministic data-chart variant): five charts.
- No Human-Scale Metaphor, Real Photo Collage, or Glass images in this package (time-boxed same-day publish; the charts carry the body).

## Cover Direction

A white receipt of identical graphite rows on warm-white paper; one warm-amber line item starts inside the receipt and runs off the paper and across the frame. No text, no numbers, no logos.

## Body Image Direction

Body images are data charts, not illustrations. Keep them quiet: warm-white ground, graphite text, neutral bars, one amber accent that always means "the thing that matters" (the $10 tier, the capped weeks, cache reads, the repriced line).

## Framework / Diagram Direction

Direct labels instead of axes and legends where possible; one message per chart stated in the title; source footnote on every chart.

## What To Avoid

Vendor logos, robots, glowing brains, dashboards, coins/credit-card clichés, generated numbers or receipt text, gradients, 3D.
