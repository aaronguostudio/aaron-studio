# Editorial Brief

## Reader Pain

“Human in the loop” sounds safe but says almost nothing about who chooses the objective, can challenge a decision, stop an action, or answer after harm.

## Reader Job To Be Done

Turn a vague concern about AI authority into a governance design that can be applied to a real consequential workflow.

## One-Sentence Promise

By the end, the reader can distinguish expertise from authority and use six institutional properties to govern an acting agent.

## Sharp Thesis

Intelligence does not authorize itself. A capable model is a high-speed expert operating inside an institution, not a philosopher king.

## Concrete Opening

Socrates was tried in 399 BCE by a large Athenian citizen jury. The episode is not a referendum, but it leaves a stubborn question: what if the people deciding lack the expertise the decision seems to require?

Name one scene, contradiction, bottleneck, result, or decision. Do not open with the topic in general.

## Original Contribution

The article turns Plato's challenge into a modern institutional answer rather than choosing democracy or technocracy: legitimacy, representation, expertise, and constraint solve different problems.

State what this article adds beyond the source material and Aaron's prior posts.

## Why Aaron Can Write This

Aaron writes from the point where agents move from answers to actions. He uses history as a carefully bounded thought partner and makes the resulting governance frame operational.

## Authority And Scope Boundary

It will not turn Athenian democracy into modern universal suffrage, claim a jury verdict was consensus or proof that democracy is false, or present India as a simple success parable.

State what Aaron knows directly, what is inferred, and what the article will not claim.

## Evidence Needed

- Credible history of Socrates' trial and jury practice.
- Primary/academic sources on Plato's democratic critique.
- Constitutional sources for India's universal adult suffrage and Ambedkar's warning about social/economic inequality.
- Existing agent-governance writing for the operational bridge.

## Counterargument

Too many controls can make a system slow and prevent useful delegation. Governance should be proportional to consequence: low-risk actions can be fast; irreversible or high-blast-radius actions need heavier constraints.

## Reusable Frame

**Delegated, bounded, visible, challengeable, reversible, accountable.** Test every consequential agent decision against these six properties.

## Distribution Hook

“Being smarter doesn't give you the right to decide.”

## Success Hypothesis

The historical opening earns a wider reader, while the six-property test gives practitioners a shareable operating frame. It concludes the series without turning earlier posts into AI preambles.

## Kill Criteria

Redraft if the article becomes a philosophy lecture, generic responsible-AI prose, or an overconfident claim that a six-word framework settles political conflict.
