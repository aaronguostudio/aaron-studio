AI governance often arrives as a reassuring phrase: “human in the loop.”

But that phrase does not tell us who gave the agent authority, what the human can see, whether anyone can challenge the outcome, or who repairs harm after a bad decision.

I ended my Greece-to-agents series with an old political question. Plato worried that power could be distributed without competence. Modern democracy's answer was not that everyone has equal expertise. It was that expertise should not automatically grant unlimited authority.

That is the right frame for agents too. A capable model is a high-speed expert layer inside an institution.

For consequential action, I want authority to be delegated, bounded, visible, challengeable, reversible, and accountable.

Where does your current agent workflow rely on presence rather than real governance?
