# Visual Strategy

## Article Character

Reflective philosophy-to-technology conclusion. Reader emotion: relief that governance can be designed, not merely announced. Risk: robot/philosopher-king cliche.

## Concept Routes

1. **A bright lantern inside a green gate** — predicate: intelligence illuminates a path but is held by an institutional boundary. Selected.
2. **A crown on a circuit board** — predicate: authority meets AI. Rejected: philosopher-king cliche.
3. **Six connected glass panels** — predicate: governance properties. Rejected: dashboard/diagram energy.

## Selection

**Agent-selected: Route 1.** It is human-scale, warm, and makes intelligence secondary to the gate that governs passage.

## Revision 2 — Decision Chamber Rather Than a Single Gate

The lantern-and-gate cover was too quiet for the series conclusion. The selected v2 cover is a circular decision chamber: one actuator and decision token sit among distinct material constraints, a human holds the outer seal, and Socrates appears as historical context rather than a philosopher-king symbol. The predicate is authority passing through governance, not intelligence walking down a road.

## Revision 3 — Greek Character Card Style Probe

The user's Greece shop references reveal a materially different visual language from v1/v2: contemporary character redesigns of classical figures, copperplate-like crosshatching, saturated single-colour grounds, ghosted myth-symbol fields, patterned clothing, and small personal objects. The rack and card holders are excluded from the reference.

Three probe routes preserve the article's governance thesis while testing distinct visual predicates:

1. **The Gatekeeper** — a contemporary philosopher-operator character holds a decision token at a green gate; predicate: authority is granted through a boundary.
2. **The Six Thresholds** — a stylized character card with six material symbols orbiting one decision seal; predicate: action must pass through constraints.
3. **The Human Seal** — a quieter portrait of a reader/guardian with an amber seal and a restrained myth-symbol field; predicate: accountability remains outside the machine.

All three are exploration assets only. `00-cover-v2` remains the active article cover until Aaron chooses a v3 direction.

## Revision 3.1 — Bright Semi-Flat Civic Greek Probe

The first v3 probes made the supplied card style too literal: they were dark, portrait-led, and their engraved rendering competed with the governance idea. The next probe set takes only the references' visual grammar — vivid Mediterranean colour, a little hatch texture, stripes, and a faint icon field — and applies it to civic places and symbolic objects.

1. **The Civic Threshold** — a sunlit, simplified Athenian public court/Agora with an empty decision seat and an open civic gate; predicate: public authority must pass through a visible boundary.
2. **The Thread Through the City** — Ariadne's red thread crosses a bright architectural labyrinth and reaches a threshold; predicate: an action remains accountable when its path is legible.
3. **The Institution in Objects** — a bright columned civic square assembled from Greek cultural objects, with a decision tablet held behind an open gate; predicate: authority is made by institutions, not by a heroic individual.

These are visual, not historical, claims. Socrates' trial is not placed at a falsely precise landmark; Greek myth is a vocabulary for the image, not a new subject for the series. All three remain exploration assets; v2 stays active.

## Revision 4 — Part IV Body Illustration Set

Aaron selected the clean F2 direction and requested enough images to test the article as a visual reading experience. The chosen cohesion model is **Unified**: bright semi-flat civic print, with a clean background and one simple institutional relation per image. This is a body-image expansion, not a mythology series or a gallery of Greek decoration.

1. **Civic Layers** — a four-level open civic structure supports one decision place; predicate: legitimacy, delegation, competence, and constraints work together rather than collapsing into one authority.
2. **Capability Is Not Authority** — a small capable tool works inside a clearly bounded tabletop; predicate: an agent may act competently without possessing the final decision token.
3. **Visible Safeguards** — six distinct civic instruments sit around one decision tablet; predicate: governance is a set of actionable controls, not the vague presence of a human.

The reader would lose: the intuition that democracy is architecture, the distinction between agent competence and authority, and the concrete feel of the six governance properties.

## Revision 5 — Bright Material Style Probes

Aaron's correction is that the clean F2 system has become too flat. The goal is not to reintroduce dark mood or background decoration; it is to regain material presence inside a bright visual field. All four probes hold the same civic still-life predicate so the comparison is about visual language rather than changing the idea.

1. **Sunlit Intaglio** — layered crosshatching, etched line weight, worn warm paper, and limited bright inks; texture comes from linework.
2. **Aegean Fresco** — lime-plaster ground, mineral pigments, softened painted edges, and shallow weathering; texture comes from wall/paint material.
3. **Mediterranean Risograph** — visible ink grain, imperfect colour registration, halftone and overprint; texture comes from print process.
4. **Paper and Textile Collage** — fibrous torn paper, woven stripe fragments, and restrained cast layers; texture comes from assembled materials.

Shared guardrails: bright cream/sky-blue field, no decorative icon wallpaper, no people, no fake text, no dark poster mood. All are exploration candidates only.
