# Idea

## Topic

**Being Smarter Doesn't Give You the Right to Decide.** From Socrates' trial and Plato's challenge to modern democratic institutions and AI-agent governance.

## Why Now

AI systems are crossing from answering into deciding and acting: they can change records, send messages, spend money, merge code, and deploy. Capability now arrives before governance language is settled.

## Target Reader

Leaders, builders, and citizens who suspect that "human in the loop" is too vague to govern consequential agents.

## Reader Pain / Curiosity

The reader sees two bad extremes: crowd judgment can be uninformed, while expert or model judgment can be overconfident, biased, and unanswerable.

## Initial Thesis

Expertise should inform decisions but never authorize itself. A consequential agent must operate inside an institution that makes authority delegated, bounded, visible, challengeable, reversible, and accountable.

## Why This Should Exist

This is the series payoff. It does not claim an AI model is a philosopher king; it argues that a capable model is a high-speed expert whose authority still has to be designed.

## Kill Criteria

Stop if Plato becomes a caricature, Socrates' jury is presented as a referendum, India becomes a decorative moral example, or the article lands on generic "responsible AI" language instead of an operational governance frame.
