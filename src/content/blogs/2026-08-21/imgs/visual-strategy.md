# Visual Strategy

## Article Character
Cost-structure operator essay; the argument is arithmetic, not atmosphere. Visual job: make the crossover legible as a single event — the meter stops; the environment remains.

## Concept Routes Compared

### Route A — Receipt Stop (SELECTED)
**Predicate:** the itemized bill is interrupted by a single stroke; past it, only the environment (machine) remains.
- A1: receipt card with item lines, one safety-orange stop line + seal, dotted leader to a minimal die (integrity preserved through the handoff).
- A2: same receipt; the tail after the stop line dissolves into a dither field that drifts toward the die (the invoice is what dies; the machine takes over).
- Why selected: one predicate, no text, no generic AI-glow, matches Field Signal Editorial (warm paper, graphite, one safety color). A2's dissolution shows death + continuation in one gesture and fills the frame with movement; A1 stays flatter.
- Risk: abstract item bars might read as "placeholder skeleton." Mitigated by scale (they are 60% of the left third, clearly an object) and the single orange interruption carrying the meaning.

### Route B — Two Curves (rejected)
Cost curves crossing: recurring meter line vs amortized purchase line. Honest and quantitative, but Data-Poster territory; a chart wall risks unreadability at blog-header size and duplicates the article's own math. The reader will find the curves in the text where they can be argued.

### Route C — Workbench Before/After (rejected)
Human-scale metaphor: cloud desk with paper bill vs local machine running. Warm but two-scene (two predicates), borderline stock-photo energy, and it would render "AI on a desk" cliché. Rejected.

## Style Decision
Cohesion: Unified Field Signal Editorial (paper-white/cream, graphite structure, exactly one safety color — orange-224,92,40 — carried by the stop line and seal). No glass, no glow, no text in the image (title belongs to the blog shell). This keeps the cover continuous with the DSH-series covers (warm restrained editorial, signal accents).
