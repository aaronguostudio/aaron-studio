## 老高与小茉 Mr & Mrs Gao (Lao Gao & Xiao Mo)

- Format: Long-form (typically ~20-35 min; runtime figure from general channel knowledge, not verified in a source) talk-show style storytelling: husband narrates a mystery/urban-legend/science-curiosity story to wife on a sofa; she plays the 捧哏 (straight-man/curious listener). Since Jan 2026 (after an Oct 2025 hiatus for their dog's illness) the channel resumed with NO on-camera presence: first voice-only over still/AI images, then a pure black-background white-subtitle 'podcast with captions' format. Weekly (Wednesday) cadence; members get ~2 extra episodes/month. ~6.7M subscribers.
- Hook pattern: Opens with the premise framed as a question or unsettling claim within the first 30-60s, delivered conversationally, then a promise that 'by the end it will get 細思極恐 (creepier the more you think)'. No cold-open montage; the hook is verbal and relies on topic selection (斩获观众的好奇心) and the partner's reaction.
- Structure: Mystery/reveal. Linear story told in escalating layers ('十五分钟下来环环深入'), each layer answering one question and opening a bigger one, ending with a twist, an open question, or a philosophical 'what if'. Partner interjections serve as built-in audience questions and comic relief. Frequently a numbered countdown within a theme (e.g., '第二名…').
- Pacing and retention: Retention comes from story escalation, not cuts. Conversational dialogue mimics a friend telling you something at dinner; they rarely look at camera, producing a 'sitting next to them listening' effect. Partner's reactions act as permission to be surprised. Extremely low edit density; minimal music. Post-2026 black-screen format shows the narration alone carries retention for this audience (though fans immediately scrutinized voice/AI authenticity).
- Visual system: Originally: static two-shot on sofa + occasional cutaway stills (maps, photos, diagrams). 2026: AI-generated stills, then black background with white captions only. Essentially an audio-first product.
- Thumbnail and title: Short, emotionally loaded Chinese titles (e.g., '可怕的石油', '2030 冰河期', '目前最細思極恐的一期，二十五號宇宙', '有些突然'). Thumbnails historically: the two hosts' expressive faces plus large yellow/white text (thumbnail detail from general channel knowledge; not verified by a fetched source).
- Narration voice: Warm, unhurried, Mandarin with Japanese-resident inflection; second person ('你想想看'); heavy use of rhetorical questions; humor via the partner. Register: curious storyteller, never lecturer.
- Production cadence and team: Weekly; two people on screen, reportedly a small team behind (not verified). Reported to earn very high revenue (Zhihu articles claim ~US$10M/yr; unverified).
- Transferable: (1) Topic selection as the primary retention lever: each episode is built on one curiosity gap with a promised payoff. (2) Escalating-layer structure ('each answer opens a bigger question') maps perfectly to agent-engineering explainers. (3) Second-person conversational script register works well with TTS and Chinese-edition scripts. (4) Proof that a Chinese-language audience will watch voice-led content with minimal visuals if the narrative is strong, which lowers the visual bar for a Chinese edition. (5) A 'virtual interlocutor' (on-screen text questions, or a second TTS voice as the curious partner) can replicate the 捧哏 device.
- Not transferable: The parasocial couple chemistry and the 7+ years of face-based trust. The 2026 faceless pivot triggered suspicion precisely because the audience had been trained on faces; a new faceless channel has no such baggage but also no such trust. The sensational/paranormal topic mix does not fit an operator-strategy channel.
- Sources: https://www.zhihu.com/question/325897604; https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E8%80%81%E9%AB%98; https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/25114204503; https://www.ctwant.com/article/468270/; https://stars.udn.com/star/story/120661/9681386; https://ent.ltn.com.tw/news/breakingnews/5420491; https://www.youtube.com/@laogao

## 小Lin说 (Xiao Lin Shuo)

- Format: Host-on-camera finance/economics explainer with PPT-like charts and animations. Flagship series '一口气了解X' (understand X in one go): 20-60 min systematic deep dives (some longer; exact durations not verified). Also question-title pieces ('胖东来，为什么走不出河南?'). Weekly-ish on YouTube/Bilibili plus Douyin cut-downs; Douyin delivers 92% of interactions from 17% of content volume (DataStory, 2025).
- Hook pattern: Cold open states the puzzle or the 'scandal/奇闻' in one sentence, often with a number, then 'today we'll 一口气 go through the whole thing'. Her early growth came from '金融奇闻' (finance oddity stories), i.e., narrative scandals, before shifting to systematic explainers.
- Structure: Thesis-proof / systematic walkthrough: timeline or mechanism broken into 4-7 chapters with on-screen chapter cards; recurring running analogy ('小Lin奶茶店' imaginary bubble-tea shop) used to model any business/economic mechanism; closes with her own judgment plus a caveat.
- Pacing and retention: High information density with humor; recurring in-jokes (奶茶店) reward returning viewers; chapter structure and 'one breath' promise reduce mid-video drop-off; story beats (who lost money, who got caught) interleaved with mechanism explanation.
- Visual system: Host talking head + clean slide-style charts, timelines, company logos, data visualizations ('就像做得很好的PPT'), occasional simple animation. Not cinematic; clarity over spectacle.
- Thumbnail and title: Titles are either '一口气了解[topic]' or a short provocative question/confrontation ('美团和京东，有完没完'). Thumbnails: her face + 2-5 large characters + a logo/graphic (pattern from channel browsing; not independently verified).
- Narration voice: Fast, articulate, self-deprecating humor, credentialed-insider register (PKU/Columbia/JPMorgan) that is explicitly part of the brand; explains with vivid analogies.
- Production cadence and team: Small team: she leads topic selection, scripting and presenting; a finance-background editor handles post; a business/ops person handles sponsors (大数跨境 analysis). Recruiting for content/video/ops roles (YouTube about page). 15M+ cross-platform followers.
- Transferable: (1) The 'one-breath systematic explainer' format is a strong fit for agent-engineering topics: chaptered, mechanism-first, with a running toy model. (2) A persistent toy-company analogy (her 奶茶店) is directly reusable as a recurring fictional firm in an operator-strategy channel and is trivially renderable as a Remotion scene template. (3) Slide-grade charts are exactly what Remotion produces well; her success shows Chinese audiences accept PPT-style visuals if the explanation is sharp. (4) Credential-as-brand: a faceless channel can substitute a clear point-of-view and named expertise in the channel description/intro card. (5) Douyin/Shorts cut-downs drive most reach; plan vertical derivatives from the start.
- Not transferable: Her on-camera charisma and insider credential signaling; the 'scandal story' content engine depends on finance having a steady supply of dramatic public failures (AI/agent engineering has fewer).

## 回形针PaperClip

- Format: Faceless, narrated, motion-graphics/3D explainer (the closest Chinese analog to the target format). Typical runtime ~8-15 min (e.g., the COVID explainer was 10m18s). Made ~80 videos in 2019 (~7/month) with a team that grew from 2 to ~20 by 2020. Channel later suspended in China after controversy (2021); useful mainly as a format benchmark.
- Hook pattern: Opens on the literal object or phenomenon with a terse question in the title and first line ('摄像头是如何监控13亿人', '如何做一根懂事的路灯'), then immediately shows the mechanism on screen. No personality preamble.
- Structure: Decomposition: a complex phenomenon is broken into constituent sub-questions, each researched from patents, national standards, textbooks and industry data, then answered sequentially with visuals carrying 'higher information density than the text'. Founder's rule: the video must not be understandable from audio alone.
- Pacing and retention: Dense, steady, serious; retention through relentless novelty of visual information and 'how does this actually work' payoff rather than jokes or cliffhangers. Skeuomorphic framing devices (old TV, phone, computer UI) to orient viewers.
- Visual system: 3D scene modeling, exploded diagrams, data overlays, skeuomorphic templates, maps. Viewers called it '百万建模/百万特效'. Consistent dark, technical, Swiss-style typography.
- Thumbnail and title: Titles are short questions using 如何/怎么/什么. Thumbnails: single clean rendered object + short text (pattern from channel; not independently verified).
- Narration voice: Flat, neutral, authoritative male narration; third person; no first-person opinions; precision over warmth.
- Production cadence and team: First video took 1 month solo (script + storyboard by founder); scaled to ~20 people producing multiple videos per week by 2020.
- Transferable: (1) The whole format: faceless narration + deterministic motion graphics + question title is exactly the target. (2) Founder's 'video information density > text density' rule is the quality bar for a Remotion pipeline: every scene should add information not present in narration. (3) Decomposition-into-sub-questions is an automatable research/outline step for a skill. (4) Short 如何/为什么 question titles work in Chinese. (5) Skeuomorphic device frames (terminal window, chat UI, browser) are a natural Remotion scene vocabulary for agent-engineering content.
- Not transferable: A 20-person 3D/motion team producing bespoke models; the one-person+AI version must rely on reusable scene templates, AI-generated stills and simple 2.5D motion instead of custom 3D. Also the mass-platform (Douyin/Bilibili) audience scale and sensitivity context.
- Sources: https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%9B%9E%E5%BD%A2%E9%92%88PaperClip; https://www.lanjinger.com/d/114274; https://36kr.com/p/1725061775361; https://finance.ifeng.com/c/7x50Mf21K6c; https://m.fx361.com/news/2021/0727/8608173.html

## Johnny Harris

- Format: Journalist-led video essays, now mostly 30-60 min deep dives (Nieman Lab, Mar 2026), with maps, archival footage, B-roll, on-camera pieces; roughly weekly output across his channels via the NewPress company.
- Hook pattern: 'Visual anchor before contextual bridge': show a striking visual or scene first ('look at this thing'), create curiosity, then explain; the title's promise is restated in the first 15-30s and a personal stake ('I went to find out') is introduced. Three-act: question/mystery, development with evidence, resolution with takeaway.
- Structure: Mystery/investigation with a personal through-line; recurring visual anchors connected by minimal contextual bridges; chapters signposted by map zooms and title cards.
- Pacing and retention: Music-driven emotional arc, frequent visual resets (map -> archive -> face -> graphic), open loops ('but that isn't the whole story'), and first-person doubt to keep stakes alive. Scripting and visual direction are done simultaneously (color-coded script notes for assets and fact-checks).
- Visual system: Signature 3D/2D map animations, archival photos with Ken Burns, hand-drawn annotations, kinetic typography, on-location footage, expert interviews.
- Thumbnail and title: Title = a bold claim or question ('Why X is…'); thumbnail = his face + one iconic image + 2-3 words, high contrast. (Pattern from channel; not source-verified.)
- Narration voice: First-person, curious, slightly breathless journalist; 'I' and 'we'; admits uncertainty; conversational.
- Production cadence and team: Large team at NewPress: ~11 animators/editors/designers, archive producers, composer, production managers (Nieman Lab 2026 list). Founded Bright Trip earlier.
- Transferable: (1) Visual-anchor-first scripting is fully implementable in a skill: for every beat, specify the visual anchor before writing the narration. (2) Write script and shot list together (one document with scene intents) — this is how a director-plan should be structured. (3) Map/diagram zoom as chapter transitions is cheap in Remotion. (4) Open-loop phrasing ('but that's not the real reason') can be encoded as a script lint.
- Not transferable: On-camera persona, travel footage, archival licensing, interviews, and a 20-person studio. His 30-60 min runtime is only justified by that production richness.
- Sources: https://chengweihu.com/io/visual-before-context/; https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/03/with-newpress-iz-and-johnny-harris-incubate-video-journalism-for-the-creator-era/; https://medium.com/@LMK_writing/how-johnny-harris-mastered-visual-storytelling-on-youtube-343ddf9160ec; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Harris_(journalist)

## Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell

- Format: Fully animated flat/3D-style science explainers, ~8-15 min, roughly 1-2 per month; ~1,200 hours per video (their own 2020 breakdown); ~77-82 employees in 2025-26; research team of ~6 (head of research, 2 fact-checkers, 2 researcher/writers, 1 social editor).
- Hook pattern: Opens with a big, often existential premise in the first 10-20s ('What if…', 'You are…'), immediately scaled to the viewer ('you'), then a promise to go 'in a nutshell'.
- Structure: Thesis-proof with escalation: premise -> mechanism explained with metaphor -> scale shock -> consequence -> reflective, often 'optimistic nihilism' ending; scripts iterated for weeks to years balancing simplicity and accuracy.
- Pacing and retention: Constant visual novelty (a new composition every few seconds), bird characters as emotional proxies, music swells at scale reveals, brief humor beats, and a consistently satisfying ending that drives binge behavior.
- Visual system: Signature flat vector illustration with a limited palette, recurring bird characters, particle/space effects; 2-3 animators spend 8-10 weeks per video.
- Thumbnail and title: Titles: short provocative claims/questions ('What if…', 'The Largest…'); thumbnails: one bold illustrated object on a flat bright background, few or no words, strong silhouette. (Widely observed; not source-verified.)
- Narration voice: Single calm British-accented narrator (Steve Taylor), second person, measured warmth, rhetorical questions, occasional dry humor.
- Production cadence and team: ~80 staff; self-funded; revenue ~$8.5M (2025 est., Latka—low confidence).
- Transferable: (1) A strict, limited visual language (palette, shapes, one illustration style) is the thing AI pipelines most lack; defining a style bible and enforcing it in image prompts/Remotion tokens is the single biggest quality lever. (2) Script-first: the script IS the product; weeks of script iteration vs. days of animation. (3) Second-person narration + scale metaphors work well with TTS. (4) Ending with a reflective takeaway, not a CTA, drives rewatch/binge. (5) Research/fact-check as a separate pipeline stage with sources listed.
- Not transferable: 1,200 hours/video, bespoke character animation, 80-person studio, proprietary visual IP. Do not try to imitate the look; imitate the discipline.
- Sources: https://kottke.org/20/02/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-just-1200-hours; https://10.studio/the-incredible-amount-of-work-behind-kurzgesagts-beautiful-animated-videos/; https://medium.com/@Kurzgesagt/how-research-and-factchecking-work-at-kurzgesagt-f5b239188255; https://tracxn.com/d/companies/kurzgesagt/__n6TOpxjft5OpWuLdQdLUWvauHJwadavs2a_VI_vGiLg; https://www.forbes.com/sites/danidiplacido/2024/10/24/kurzgesagt-in-a-nutshell-creator-talks-youtube-tiktok-and-optimistic-nihilism/

## Wendover Productions (Sam Denby)

- Format: Faceless narrated explainers on logistics/geography/economics, 10-20 min (longer recently), custom maps and infographics plus licensed footage; main channel cadence roughly 2-4/month; team of ~7 (editors, writers, producers) across two offices; Denby is also Nebula's chief content officer.
- Hook pattern: Opens on a specific, concrete place/object/number ('This is X. It is…') and a counterintuitive fact, then the systemic question it implies. Minimal throat-clearing; no personality intro.
- Structure: Thesis-proof systems explainer: setup the system -> constraints -> how actors respond -> second-order consequences -> conclusion; chaptered by map moves.
- Pacing and retention: Steady, dense, 'newsroom' cadence; retention via continual concrete specifics (named ports, prices, distances) and map-driven visual progression rather than jokes. Sourced numbers every few sentences.
- Visual system: Flat custom maps with animated routes, simple iconography, bar/line charts, stock/licensed B-roll, consistent typography; Ken Burns on photos.
- Thumbnail and title: Titles: 'How X Works', 'Why X Is So Y', 'The Logistics of…'; thumbnails: map or object with 2-4 words, consistent font. (Pattern from channel; not source-verified.)
- Narration voice: Flat, precise, deadpan ('tax attorney auditioning for noir'); third person; no filler; comma-timed pauses.
- Production cadence and team: ~7 staff; scripts described as slow to produce; weekly cadence on main channel judged incompatible with quality.
- Transferable: (1) The faceless map/infographic + flat narration model is the most directly imitable top-tier English format for Remotion: maps (Remotion has a cartography skill), charts, route animations, icon systems. (2) 'Concrete specifics every few sentences' is a script-quality rule a skill can lint for. (3) 'How X works / Why X is…' title grammar translates to agent-engineering. (4) Deadpan narration is the register TTS handles best (ElevenLabs does 'calm precise' far better than 'excited').
- Not transferable: Licensed aerial/B-roll footage budgets, a writing staff, and Nebula distribution. Also the logistics subject matter's natural visual richness (ships, planes) that AI/agent topics lack; you need invented visual metaphors instead.

## Fireship (Jeff Delaney)

- Format: Solo creator. Two formats: '100 Seconds of X' (~100s crash courses) and 'The Code Report' (~4-8 min dev-news, launched Feb 2022) plus 8-15 min deep dives. Fast, meme-dense, screen-recording + kinetic text. Cadence several per week at peak.
- Hook pattern: Cold open within 3-5 seconds: a dramatic framing of the news ('It's [date] and you're watching The Code Report') or an absurd premise, then immediate payoff; the joke and the fact land together.
- Structure: Listicle/newsreel: 3-6 items or steps, each with a visual gag, rapid wrap with a contrarian take and a sponsor read that is itself a joke.
- Pacing and retention: Extreme cut density (a visual change every 1-3 seconds), memes, deadpan irony, no pauses; runtime kept short so absolute retention is high; a consistent serial format (Code Report) trains habit.
- Visual system: Screen recordings, code snippets with animated highlights, logos, meme images/clips, simple kinetic typography on dark background; recognizable fire-logo brand; consistent show-open.
- Thumbnail and title: Titles: '[Tech] in 100 Seconds', '[Company] just did X…', provocative lower-case wit; thumbnails: logo(s) + short text, dark background, sometimes a cartoon face/meme. Very consistent series branding.
- Narration voice: Dry, fast, deadpan first-person developer; sarcasm; assumes technical literacy; 'let's be honest' asides.
- Production cadence and team: Solo (writes, records, edits); some community-sourced memes; multiple videos/week. Exact hours per video not found.
- Transferable: (1) Proof that one person can dominate a technical niche with screen recordings + text motion and no face; the Code Report's serial show-open/format is cheap to template in Remotion (a 'remotion-fireship' template exists, Feb 2026). (2) Short runtime with high cut density is the easiest way for an AI pipeline to score well on absolute retention. (3) Code/terminal/chat UI as primary visual is native to AI-agent content. (4) A consistent serial (e.g., a weekly 'agent report') builds habit.
- Not transferable: His comedic timing and meme literacy are hard to reproduce with TTS (jokes need breath and emphasis control); the sarcastic register can read as cheap when synthetic. Also, his content is news-reactive rather than editorial/strategic.

## Cross-cutting principles

- Script is the product. Every top channel (Kurzgesagt weeks-to-years of script iteration, 回形针 month-long first video, Wendover 'scripts are slow', Lemmino months) spends most effort on writing and research; animation is downstream. A one-person+AI pipeline should spend its human time on the script and push rendering to automation.
- One curiosity gap per video, stated in the first 15-30s, paid off at the end. 老高 ('細思極恐' promise), Kurzgesagt ('What if…'), Harris (visual anchor first), Lemmino (single enigma) all do this; YouTube's Creator Insider guidance is to establish value within ~7 seconds and benchmarks put strong 30-second retention at >75%.
- Escalating-layer structure beats listicles for long-form: each answer opens a bigger question (老高, Harris, Kurzgesagt). Listicles work only for short serial formats (Fireship Code Report).
- Visual information density must exceed narration density (回形针's rule): every scene should add something not in the audio. This is the strongest single differentiator from AI slop, where visuals merely decorate narration.
- A strict, limited visual system (Kurzgesagt palette, Lemmino dark-grey + 3 colors, Fireship dark code theme, Wendover flat maps) is what makes a channel recognizable; AI pipelines fail mostly on inconsistency, so define a style bible and enforce it as tokens in Remotion and image prompts.
- Pick a narration register TTS can actually do: calm third-person (Lemmino, Wendover, 回形针) or warm second-person (Kurzgesagt, 老高). Avoid sarcasm/comic timing (Fireship) and breathless first-person (Harris) unless you can direct emphasis word-by-word.
- Concrete specifics every few sentences (names, numbers, dates, sources) and visible citations build trust for a faceless channel (Wendover, Lemmino, Kurzgesagt research team). This is also what YouTube's inauthentic-content policy implicitly rewards: original framing and human-added value.
- Recurring devices create a 'show': a running toy company (小Lin's 奶茶店), bird mascots (Kurzgesagt), a show-open (Code Report), chapter cards (Lemmino). A faceless channel needs at least one such device to substitute for a face.
- Titles are short questions or claims: 如何/为什么/什么 (回形针, 小Lin), 'How X works / Why X is…' (Wendover), 'What if…' (Kurzgesagt). Thumbnails: one object, strong silhouette, 2-4 words, consistent typography; faces are optional (Kurzgesagt, Lemmino, Wendover, 回形针 all succeed without them).
- Plan vertical cut-downs from the start: 小Lin gets 92% of interactions from Douyin; Fireship's 100-second format is Shorts-adjacent. Runtime sweet spot for a new faceless editorial channel is 8-15 min (回形针, Kurzgesagt, Wendover) rather than 30-60 (Harris, Lemmino, 老高) until retention data says otherwise.
- Audience-retention benchmarks to aim for (third-party analyst figures, moderate confidence): platform-wide average ~24%; 'good' for 10-15 min videos 40-50%; first-30-seconds retention >75% strong, 60-75% average; CTR 4-6% typical for established channels, higher for small channels with targeted impressions.

## AI-native explainer landscape

State of play 2025-2026: (1) Tooling commoditized. Typical faceless stacks are ChatGPT/Claude for scripts, ElevenLabs for voice, Pictory/Virvid/InVideo/Runway for visuals, Canva/CapCut for edit; guides quote $47-180/month stacks and '30 minutes per video' (virvid.ai, Feb 2026). Rendering/assembly APIs (JSON2Video, Creatomate, Shotstack, Remotion Lambda) make batch production trivial. (2) Quality ceiling of the mass market is low and recognizably 'slop': same synthetic voice, stock-feeling AI footage, identical script template with variables swapped, generic transitions, interchangeable hooks, no persona anchoring the channel (kompozy.io, outlierkit 2026). 'AI slop' was Merriam-Webster/ADS word of the year 2025. (3) Platform response: YouTube renamed 'repetitious content' to 'inauthentic content' on July 15, 2025 (mass-produced slideshows with the same narration, templated narrative stories demonetized); a further clarification in mid-July 2026 targeted generic/repetitive content, manipulative shock content, and AI personas posing as human experts. AI-assisted channels with original framing and human editorial value remain monetizable; enforcement examples include full channel removals. (4) What the few good ones do differently (synthesis across analyst posts and the creator benchmarks above): a real editorial point of view and domain expertise; script quality with concrete specifics; a consistent, deliberate visual system rather than per-scene random AI imagery; voice direction (pacing, pauses, emphasis) instead of default TTS; a recurring persona/device; and niche depth. Tech/AI commentary, macro and geopolitics are reported as the most profitable faceless niches. (5) Code-driven motion graphics (Remotion + Claude Code) is the emerging 'premium faceless' lane: lower-thirds, title cards, animated diagrams and data viz generated from code, consistent across hundreds of videos, editable by changing one file (Medium, Jun 2026; Remotion skill 440K installs by Aug 2026). (6) Retention/CTR benchmarks are only available from third-party analysts (Lenos, thumbnailinsight, virvid, teleprompter.com, 2025-26), not YouTube official: platform average retention ~23.7%, strong 40-50% for 10-15 min; >75% at 30s is strong; 'pattern-matched' hooks (intro delivers the title's promise) retain ~78% through 30s vs. 40%+ loss in 5s for mismatched hooks (1of10 claim, unverified methodology); CTR 4-6% typical. YouTube's own Creator Insider advises establishing value within ~7 seconds. Confidence: moderate on policy dates (multiple sources agree), low-to-moderate on benchmark numbers (analyst estimates).
